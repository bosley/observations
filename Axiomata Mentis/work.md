# Forward 

The following statements will be made to guide the reader through the same self-inflicted expereinces sequentially to demonstrate the conditions for which the statement was made in the hopes of illuminating my particular picture of the world. 

I will not rely on external material to pin my ideas on others, for reasons that will become apparent after digesting this material; I don't want to repeat myself. 

# Starting condition 

When I'm by myself sitting still, comfortable and unbothered by hunger, thirst, or any other bodily need and completly disconnect from any physical object such as my phone, a television, or radio, with my eyes shut, I'm at peace. 

When I'm sitting still like this, and let my awareness shrink from the concerns of my life, job, and all physical affairs, I have no thoughts.

# 1.0 - I exist, and I'm in an environment

Paying attention to the parts of my body: aches in the feet and back, the slightly-too-warm heat from the vent exacerbating the cold on the other side, the shape my hands rest in, tension in a neck muscle.

No thoughts about the feelings, just feeling. Letting the body tell you a story through the soft sensations imposed by the environment.

In this moment the only thing I am aware of is that "I exist, and I'm in an environment."

## 1.1

This informs me that there is some "me" that "has a body" and is "in an environment." Why am I experiencing this as a separation from the body? What differentiates the feeling from a feeler?

### 1.11 

If I cut my hair, I'm still me. If I cut off my arm, I'm still me, provided I survive.

Following the logic of removal sans fatality: I am a brain in a body, supported by the systems of that body.

### 1.12

We suspect the carrier of the "I" is the brain. Through unfortunate circumstances we've swapped hearts, kidneys, and other organs without anyone proving to have become someone else.

Modern science says the brain triggers movement and hosts the memories and other structures that constitute the self. This seems sensible given personality changes and loss of memory with brain damage.

### 1.13

All I can say with certainty is that I've witnessed the processing power and modality of my mind fluctuate with the consumption of materials. However it works, the "I" I experience is reliant on my body.

I can't say from my own experience that my mind ceases when the body dies. I can say the body, without the mind, would surely die. It wouldn't obtain the resources necessary to function, and it would run out of energy.

## 1.2

The environment, the stage that everything happens. Its where temperature, objects, and even other beings reside. Perhaps not within the confines of the walls I've surrounded myself with, but I've seen objects that look not unlike myself in form, walk and talk and claim to "be" as I do. 

### 1.21

The other beings in this environment are of my same kind, so I assume for the sake of operation that they too hold a space of abstractions, notions, and feelings similar enough to my own that through some means or another I can place something I've thought of into their mind.

To do so I must cross the gap imposed by the environment between us, the very thing that seems to render us distinct.

I can move parts of my body to vibrate the gas in the environment.

I can't see into their space to watch what the vibration becomes. To tie a vibration to a concept I could present that mind with an object and vibrate the environment so they correlate the object and the vibration, then do the same across enough objects that each vibration is correlated to one object.

Then I could compose those objects into sequences and map the occurrence of those sequences to further vibrations.

Over time it might be easier to make a series of etchings in the environment, on some surface, mapped to the noises I use to represent the objects and the sequences. The etching remains after I stop vibrating the gas. By it I can tell them about things I don't have on hand, and they need not have been there when the noise was made.

This is language.
