
# Section 9

The phenomenon of weight is observable in any case where a stimulus of low informational complexity produces a response disproportionate to its face value. A common mild example is an olfactory trigger — a scent associated with a childhood memory producing an emotional response whose intensity is not accounted for by the scent itself, but by the accumulated consequence written into the soft system during the original encounter. The scent is neutral to an observer who carries no such writing. To the mind that does, it is a trigger.

At the more extreme end of the spectrum, clinical literature on trauma documents cases where a stimulus that was incidentally present during a high-consequence event — a sound, a smell, a texture, a word — becomes sufficient on its own to release a response of the same magnitude as the original event. The stimulus need not resemble the event in any structural way. Its only relevant property is that it was present. Over time, and with sufficient depth of writing, the trigger can become increasingly abstract: a representation of the stimulus, or a word denoting it, may carry the same releasing function as the sensory original. This is consistent with the observation in 9.03 that the more extreme the original consequence, the more abstract the trigger can become while still releasing the full response.

At the upper limit, where the writing crosses from the soft system into the hard system (9.031), the response is no longer purely behavioral or emotional. It becomes somatic — physical symptoms arising from a stimulus with no physical causal relationship to those symptoms. The body responds as though the original event is occurring. This is the hardened end of the spectrum: a writing so deep that it is no longer subject to the same revision processes available to the soft system.
