Our Present Condition

As individuals we eventually come across "the big questions" that we sometimes have installed reasonings for depending on our familial upbringings. These questions are the ones that can be the source of our meaning in every instance of every day, and can also be the muse to our bitterness.

"Why are we here?" "What is the point to life?" "What should I do with my life?" "What is life?"

Since the dawn of the first men and women these questions have been filled in by observations derived and distilled from reflections of ourselves onto the forces in the environments around us. In early antiquity these were personifications of distinct existential threats and the groupings of like-attributes around names that allowed the minds at the time to create narratives that helped guide their progressions through the monotony of daily life by offering a sort of guiding light, or set of lights, that helped inform and align multiple disparate individuals towards shared ideals and understandings of "what is right to do" based on the narratives.

Sometimes the conclusions of "what is right" would seem abhorrent to the modern mind as they have, in some cases, been justifications for human sacrifice and a myriad of similar atrocities.
Over time as mankind's projections onto reality helped them tame themselves against their innate animal nature, some of the regions of man coalesced the various gods into aspects of a singular god as has happened in what is now considered "the west" or "western civilization."
 
In continuing with the process of minimization, and through post-modern deconstructionalism, it has been noted by some that we have now killed god entirely. What does this mean? It means that through our societal structure we have now been able to "make sense of the world" without needing to consider god or the gods at all. This independence has drawn some men into pure madness in existential woes. Existential anguish and despair is thought to be a byproduct of staring into the undergird of reality and no longer seeing someone looking back, but in truth these woes are downstream not of dead gods but the disenchantment with our condition of being that came through the process of killing them, a process that every non-religious human does either intentionally or as consequence of being raised in the west and through the worldview installed into them by their proximal influences.

What is this disenchantment? It’s the killing off of personality through the process of systematizing all phenomena into “sensible models.” The models themselves need not be held by the person who loses the mystery. It is enough that the knowledge that the forces have already been understood by someone sits as common fact. Wind that takes a roof, tide that moves a line of posts, storm that chooses one field and leaves the next, these used to remain active unknowns, things a person could still address, blame, or treat as having temper. People reflected some aspect of themselves onto them so the unknown could be faced. The knowledge that the forces are accounted for and not personal ends that.

What is left then? An individual in the same sea of troubles and kinds of circumstances that have been with us since the dawn of our being, but now are left with a seemingly logical conclusion of "victimhood." We are simply like the cliff next to the sea. Some of us are stronger and can withstand more erosion, while some are made of sand and fall away with the slightest of storms. 
Some people in this situation gain a sense of strength and hardening by reorganizing their foundational substrate through adoption of narratives through dead institutions or by ad-hoc crafting of a Frankenstein reconstructionalist set of beliefs based on their understandings of "what people used to believe." 

While this can make an individual solid enough to withstand troubles until their final fall into the ocean (death) it is not any indication of the truthiness of their narratives, but simply not the truthiness of the narratives is not what hardens their foundation.
