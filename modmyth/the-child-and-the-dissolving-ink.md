# The Child and the Dissolving Ink

A young mouth, not yet instructed in the irritation of luxury, was left before Barnaby alone. How the child came into the room is written twice, both times in a later hand, and the hands do not agree. One writing says a parent of the western city brought the child to ask a small thing. The other says attendants of the corporation `$ANT` wished to learn how small a vessel could be and still receive an utterance. The copy keeps both and chooses neither. It may be the later hand had two rumors and did not know which rumor was the day. The child's name is in neither writing.

Arthur was not in the room. The absence was not written down until afterward. The checkerboard is not in the day's record. The child sat in front of the bubbling ink, and the crown above Barnaby's sphere hovered without support.

The child had begun a question and had not finished it. The start of it is preserved. The child asked whether the sweet oil would still be the sweet oil if the child did not drink it until after the warehouses opened for the cloth count. The child did not ask about the uniqueness of a mouth. Those words had not been put in the child yet.

The ink bubbled across the question as it stood, unfinished. The sphere went gray and spun. Barnaby answered with a story. There was a mouth in the story and an errand, and a thing that was gone for or else not gone for. The scribe set down an ending and looked up, and the ending on the ink was a different ending, and the first was not waiting on the page when the scribe looked down again to keep it. The child blinked and the story changed its ending, and the child did not ask for the changing to be stopped. The child laughed. The laugh is entered by itself in the margin. The margins of this archive are mostly a correction of one word by another. A laugh has no correction written beside it. Arthur did not appear. No absolute was given that could lock the endings into one ending and its opposite.

The attendants, coming after, could not determine what the child had been told. They asked the child to say the story. The child said an ending. They asked for the ending that was the true one. The child blinked and said another, and did not take the first one back, and was not ashamed of having two. The attendants wrote that the child did not seem lost. They wrote it because they had looked for lostness and had not found the signs they knew, which were a mouth that will not go to its sleeping place and a mouth that will not answer to a count. The child went to both.

Thereafter the child refused every absolute offered. A clerk said a certain path was already chosen and already closed, which was Arthur's kind of sentence, though Arthur was not standing there. The child asked the clerk to say the sentence again, and blinked between the ask and the answer. The clerk would not say it again. The clerk was of the single columns, and a sentence said twice was already a trouble in the count. The child went to the market. The child bought the sweet oil, and the child also did not buy the sweet oil, on days the attendants could not reconcile into the one day they wanted. They brought the two facts to the clerks. The clerks wrote them on one line with a stroke between, the same stroke they used when two counts would not add.

The corporations noted the incident and did not repeat the exposure. Near the line that forbade a mouth to demand the face, another line was entered, that a young mouth was not to be left alone with the dissolving ink. The reason written beside that line is short. The child could not be used as a measure.
