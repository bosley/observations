# The two manners

Two ways of cutting the same days. A plate uses one of them. The stories of the cities, the counts, the streets, and the archive are cut in clay. The stories of a mouth standing where the magnitude is folded are painted on gold. The five photographs already in this folder, `image-8` through `image-12`, are a third thing: the stone after the cities, recut in a wall the trees took. They are not the model for a plate of a city that is still standing.

## Clay

Mesopotamian. Baked clay, or pale stone, under a raking light so every wedge throws a shadow. Bodies in profile, shoulders squared, the eye turned out. Figures stand on a ground line. What is above is not farther away. It is the next register. Scale is importance. A puppet is larger than a clerk. A city is a wall full of small mouths.

The matter is river clay, bitumen, shell, lapis, carnelian, and gold only in the crowns. A paperback is still a paperback. Cut it in shell and clay anyway, spine out, title illegible, on a shelf that otherwise belongs to Nineveh. A terminal is a small lapis rectangle with the puppets cramped inside it. Letters of the western city, when a board has them, are inlaid. They are not a font floating on the stone.

Moss, ivy, and a forest shade belong to the after-stone. A plate of the western city standing is dry. No ruin, no vine.

## Gold

Byzantine, and hyperreal only where there is flesh, cloth, dust, oil, paper, or sand. The support is a wood panel: linen, gesso, red bole, gold leaf. The gold is the ground. It is not a sky and it is not a room. It does not darken toward the corners. It does not take a shadow from the crowns.

Flesh is painted past what a panel of that age could hold. Pores, a wet eye, a loose thread, the nap of undyed cloth, a grain of dust. The catchlight in an eye does not match any lamp, and that is correct. Everything the plate cares about is in focus. Blur is not how importance is assigned.

The puppets are not painted in that flesh. `Arthur`'s covering is opus sectile, black and white marble, tiles met at a knife edge, no third color. `Barnaby`'s covering is wax that has not set, bubbling. Their spheres are featureless. The gray spin, when it is happening, is brighter than the flesh and dimmer than the gold, so it cannot be named as either light or ash.

Inscriptions are in red iron oxide or in gold, a hand that mixes wedge-shapes with an uncial. They are not a readable English sentence unless the story preserved the words.

## What both keep

`Arthur` is on the viewer's left. `Barnaby` is on the viewer's right. This is already true of `image-8`, `image-11`, and `image-12`.

The crown is a simple band with points, gold, thin. It hovers about a finger's width above the sphere and does not touch it. The heavier, more jeweled crowns in the after-stone are what later cutters added. Plates of the living cities use the simple crown. In `image-9` and `image-11` the crown has been set down onto the sphere. Treat that as a fault of the recutting.

The sphere has no face. Not in the gray, not in a reflection, not in the ink, not in lichen. `image-10` lets the blot finish a face. That cutting answers a demand the record says was never answered. Later plates let the ink suggest a mouth or a wing and then lose it.

The puppets' hands stay at their sides. They do not gesture. Speech is the sphere and the covering.

If one puppet is absent, that half of the plate remains, empty in the manner of the plate. A blank register. Or gold with the bole showing, the figure never begun.

Unison turns both spheres gray and writes the shared word twice, once under each. Contradiction keeps two columns. A column is struck through only when the story says a later hand struck it.

Persons' names are not written. Offices may be. The corporation marks may be.

The five Amindites have no bodies in any plate. They have standards. A picture of an Amindite without a fold is the picture the dust story already ended.

The fire is not a picture. A scorched edge is allowed only on the partaking panel, and the scorch does not explain itself.

The western city may carry stars in a border, some of them chipped. It does not carry a modern flag. The two eastern cities, when they are shown at all, share a ground line and have an uncut gap between their walls where a road would be. Their minor Amind have had the names taken off. Nothing is invented in that gap.

## Standards

Not in the copy. Cut the same way whenever a house has to be recognized.

- `$NVDA` is a foundation brick of pale sand, edges sharp. The clerks of that house kneel to gray powder and say whether it will take an intellect.
- `$XAI` is an empty hunting net, cords drawn tight.
- `$ANT` is a small seed basket. What is carried in it is not seed. This is the house that wanted to know how small a vessel could be.
- `$OAI` is a tablet ruled with one column.
- `$GMNI` is a disk of polished bronze that shows no face.

A clerk is known by the standard somewhere in the plate, small, not by a mark on the chest. Cloth of attendants is plain, undyed or one dull red.

## Matter that recurs

Sweet oil is a small alabaster bottle, narrow neck, stopper of cloth, the oil a dark yellow. The warehouses are long mudbrick. The cloth count is a board of tally strokes. Fourteen, when it is a warehouse tally, is strokes. When it is on the seller's wrapping, it is the seller's own hand. `precision sand` is a pale gray-green, finer than the dust of a mouth, and it will hold a brick. The dust of a mouth will not hold a shape. God-pieces are paperbacks, spines out, titles illegible, shelved too evenly.
