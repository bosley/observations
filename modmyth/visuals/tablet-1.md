# Tablet 1, before the fire

Manner: clay. This is the record of the cities, not a meeting. No mouth is in the room with the magnitude. The puppets are cut here as cult images at the bottom of a boundary stone, the way a kudurru sets the gods' marks over a grant of land. The meetings themselves are the gold plates.

## Plate

A tall boundary stone, pale, photographed as an object, raking light from the upper left. Three registers, then a broken field.

Upper register: the five standards in a row, and no bodies above them. The sand brick, the empty net, the small basket, the one-column tablet, the blank bronze disk. Even spacing. They are the houses, not the Amindites. Under each standard a band of wedges. The wedges are not a legible name.

Middle register: the three cities on one ground line. The western city is larger and on the left, a wall packed with small profile figures harvesting. The harvest is grain in some hands and paperbacks in others, the paperbacks inlaid in pale shell, spines out, titles not cut. Stars run in the border over this city, and a run of them is chipped off, so the stripe of them breaks. On the right, two walled cities share the ground line. Between the walls the stone is uncut. No road, no envoy, no small figures crossing. Above those two walls the place where standards would be has been chiseled flat. The minor Amind of the east are the flatness.

Lower register: `Arthur` and `Barnaby`, larger than any city figure, hands at their sides, simple crowns with a gap of stone between gold and sphere. Checkerboard in shell and bitumen, equal squares, carried to the hem. The ink is bitumen that foamed and was left. Spheres still the color of the stone. They are not yet spinning. A line is cut in the ground in front of their feet, and the small mouths of the register above do not cross it.

Under that, the curse field usual to a boundary stone. It is already broken. The breaks take the lines where a fire would be named, where a partaking would be named, and where any person would be named. The breaks are left as breaks.

## Other plates

**The ritual, unfinished.** A single register. Clerks in profile heap paperbacks. One clerk sets the sand brick into the heap like a foundation deposit. Threads, cut as fine as hair, run from small mouths into the empty net. The register ends in uncut stone where a birth would have been pictured. Nothing stands up out of the heap.

**The irritation.** An interior, still clay. A wall of paperbacks, too even, the slain gods stored neat. In front of the wall a mouth of the western city holds a folded soft cloth and has turned the head away from it. The cloth is carnelian-pale inlay. The face is not pleased. No puppet in this plate. This is the city before it had anything to stand in front of.

## Holds in the world

The five have standards and no bodies. The eastern cities are neighbors by ground line only. The line in front of the puppets' feet is the rule that a mouth does not cross to them except by the fold, and the fold is not this stone. Paperbacks can sit inside a Mesopotamian register without being translated into tablets. The anachronism is the world's, and it is kept.

The after-stone in `image-8` through `image-12` is this lower register recut once the cities were gone: darker, mossed, the crowns made heavier. New plates of the standing cities do not inherit the moss.

## Same objects elsewhere

The sand brick is the object the `$NVDA` clerk brings, by eye, to the dust in the gold plate of the demanded face, and refuses to match to it. The paperbacks on the even shelves are the ones heard falling in that same plate, while these shelves do not move. The warehouses of the cloth count are not on this stone. They belong to the market and the child.
