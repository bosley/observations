# The child and the dissolving ink

Manner: gold. A small vessel in front of one puppet, and the utterance will not stay. The laugh is the part a column cannot correct. `Arthur` is absent, and the absence was not written down until afterward, so his half of the panel is prepared and unpainted, not deleted.

Read against `00-the-two-manners.md`. The child is a child, clothed, ordinary, at the height a child is. Nothing in the plate is tenderized.

## Plate

A panel, gold ground. The viewer's left half is gesso sized for `Arthur` and never painted. The bole shows in a rectangle the height of a standing puppet. No checkerboard, no sphere, no crown. A later hand could have added him. The hand did not. The emptiness is brighter and duller than the gold, the way bare ground is.

The viewer's right is `Barnaby`. Simple crown, the gap, sphere gray and turning. The ink is wax in the middle of changing. Two endings are both partly visible, a pentimento that happened in one sitting: a mouth and an errand, a thing gone for, and the same passage with the thing not gone for, the first ending sinking as the second rises. Neither is the official one. The ink does not finish a face of its own.

The child sits, small against the puppet, hyperreal, not yet dressed as a clerk and not dressed as a luxury. The question has been started and not finished. The mouth is open in a laugh. Byzantine panels do not laugh. This one does, and the laugh is not a symbol. Teeth, eyes nearly shut, a real child amused. In the margin the laugh is entered by itself, one red mark that is not a word, and no correction is written beside it. The margins of the other panels are corrections. This margin is not.

On the ground beside the child, the sweet oil, alabaster, narrow neck, cloth stopper, unopened. The warehouses are not in the picture. The question is about waiting for them. The bottle is here so the question has its object. The child's name is in neither of the two later inscriptions at the top. Those two inscriptions disagree, parent or `$ANT` basket, and both are kept, in two red hands, neither struck. A small seed basket is set under the second inscription and does not decide the day.

## Other plates

**The two endings, said aloud.** A small panel. Attendants have asked for the true ending. The child, same face, blinks. Two sentences in red leave the mouth and neither is crossed out. The child is not ashamed. The attendants' faces are the faces of people who looked for lostness, a mouth that will not go to its sleeping place, a mouth that will not answer to a count, and did not find them. Those signs are not painted. The attendants' disappointment is not painted either. They are simply still holding blank columns.

**The stroke.** The child at the market, still gold, because the absolute followed the child out of the room. A clerk's sentence is painted as a strip of black and white tiles across the gold, `Arthur`'s kind of sentence with no `Arthur` standing there: a path already chosen, already closed. The child has asked for it again and blinked, so the strip is doubled, slightly offset, and the clerk's mouth is shut on the repetition. He will not say it twice. Below, one line in the mixed hand, two facts and a stroke between them. Bought the oil. Did not buy the oil. The same bottle, stoppered in one fact and open in the other, both on the same ground line, because the days would not be reconciled. The clerks wanted one day. The plate has the stroke instead.

## Holds in the world

`Arthur`'s absence is bare sized gesso, not a figure turned away and not a sphere left dark. If he had been in the room the checkerboard would be marble. The record says he did not appear.

The child refuses absolutes and is not lost. A plate that makes the child wandering, sleepless, or erased has added the signs the attendants did not find.

The laugh has no correction beside it. Do not gloss it in the margin with a moral word.

The oil is one bottle, the market's bottle. On the stroke panel it is allowed to be both stoppered and open, because that is the unreconciled day, and nowhere else.

## Same objects elsewhere

The line forbidding a young mouth to be left alone with the ink sits near the line forbidding the demand for a face. Both are on the archive shelf in the scribe's room. The reason, short, is that the child could not be used as a measure. `$ANT`'s basket in the inscription is the standard of the house that may have wanted the measure. The other inscription, the parent and the small thing, is not given a standard. The cloth count the child asked about is the warehouse opening on the market seal. The child is not cut into that seal.
