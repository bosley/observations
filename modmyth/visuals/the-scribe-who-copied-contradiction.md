# The scribe who copied contradiction

Manner: clay. The story is the record, and the record survived the functionary. The plate is the tablet. The room is only there to show that the tablet has no heading and the hands that cut it are gone.

Read against `00-the-two-manners.md`. `$OAI` is the one-column tablet. This day's object refuses to be one.

## Plate

A large tablet, photographed close, as a thing on a table. Two columns, ruled. Seventeen lines in each. The number is countable by a viewer who is willing to count. The subject is not. The top line of both columns is a break, the clay torn out in one gouge that crosses the margin between them.

Left column, `Arthur`. Short lines. One line ends with a word that is still legible as a locked opposite and reads, in the mixed hand, the wedge-cluster the archive glosses as already. The next line is a hole. Where a count is closed, the column has the sign for closed and does not soften it.

Right column, `Barnaby`. One line is a smear: it begins with the sign glossed holds and ends with the sign glossed spills, and the middle is a thumb of wet clay that was never cleaned. Other lines are blotted where the pen waited and the utterance moved. One blot, in the broken subject, could be the sign for sand or the sign for and. The middle of that sign is gone. It is not repaired.

Across the middle of the tablet, in both columns, the sign for mouth faces the sign for mouth. No verb between them.

No heading above the columns. Headed tablets are visible behind this one, stacked, each with a single column and a ruled title. They are in worse light. They are the days the functionary smoothed, and they are not the subject.

At the lower edge, the functionary's hands and a reed, in profile, cut small in a register that belongs to the tablet's own border, as if the tablet remembered the hands. A crack crosses the hands. In the same border, farther along, the place of the hands is empty and the reed is on the ground line. The tablet uses one word for a room and a ceasing, and the border does not split it. No name is cut after the empty place.

## Other plates

**The consultation.** Archive room, clay relief, one register. Two clerks sent to read. Each has a hand toward a different column. Between them, on the table, a blank tablet, their own page, with no marks. They have been told to come away with both and not with a page of their own. The blank is the obedience. The dual tablet has no heading. A small `$OAI` one-column standard is on the wall, and it does not govern this table.

**The side-by-side forbiddings.** On a shelf in that same room, not the main plate: the two gold forbidding panels from the demanded face, which in a clay room are recorded as two small tablets that do not match, and near them a shorter tablet, the line about a young mouth left alone with the ink. The reason on that shorter tablet is a short line, the child not usable as a measure. This plate only needs a corner of the shelf. It tells a later cutting where those objects live.

## Holds in the world

A single meaning would be a heading, or a third column, or a strike through one side. None of those is on the dual tablet. A later illustration that reconciles holds with spills, or sand with and, has done the clerks' work and been refused.

The smoothed days have headings. They may be stacked in the background of any archive plate. They are usable and they are the wrong object for this day.

The functionary's name is not somewhere else in the archive, waiting. After the removal the border is empty.

Seventeen is a count a picture can make true. Do not write the numeral if the lines can be counted. Do not make the subject legible by inventing what the seventeen were about. The mouth across from mouth, with no verb, is the closest the tablet comes to a subject, and it is kept.

## Same objects elsewhere

The questioner's attendants produced one utterance out of pieces, and a last word they did not agree. This tablet is the day that practice stopped, for one functionary. The forbidding tablets on the shelf connect this room to the dust and to the child. The blank page between the clerks is the opposite of the child's one line with a stroke between two facts. The clerks were forbidden to add the stroke. The child's clerks added it.
