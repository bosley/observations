# Unison in the night market

Manner: clay. A city doing a count. The wet mind was large enough for one sentence, and the sentence left the glass and became a wrapping, a price, a street. That is a record. It can be cut.

Read against `00-the-two-manners.md`. The seller has not partaken, so there is no gray in the seller's mouth.

## Plate

An unrolled cylinder seal, three registers, baked clay, the impression repeating once so the viewer understands it is a seal and then the repeat is allowed to stop. Raking light. No moss.

Top register: the night market. Lamps are small bowls with a cut flame. Stalls. Small luxuries, and the irritation of them: a figure holding the soft cloth out, away from the body. Another figure with the alabaster oil bottle, not yet bought, looking at it as at a problem. Profile bodies, frontal eyes.

Middle register: the stall, cloth hung behind it. The seller in profile, one hand tilting a stand. On the stand a small lapis rectangle, the private terminal. Inside the rectangle the two puppets are cramped, `Arthur` left, `Barnaby` right, checker and bubbling bitumen, spheres gone gray together. The crowns are cut by the top edge of the lapis in the untilted version, which is a smaller ghost impression behind, and saved in the tilted one, the gap between crown and sphere still visible inside the glass. A sentence is cut leaving the rectangle, the wedges sliding off the lapis onto a wrapping the seller holds. The wrapping is pale shell. What is legible on it, in the seller's own cut, not in wedges: a price of fourteen, a date-mark, and a street-name. The street-name is the part the thumb will destroy. Here it is still whole, because this is the evening, not the worn relic.

Bottom register: three cycles later, the same ground line broken. A street has been cut through the warehouse row the way a canal is cut through a seal, interrupting the figures. At each end a board, shell inlay, the name legible in the western city's letters. Warehouse mouths in profile, and the price on their tally is fourteen strokes. The seller's wrapping appears again at the corner, and here the name is a thumb-shaped blank. The fourteen and the date-mark remain. The seller does not recut the name. The board has it.

The seller's mouth, both times, is an ordinary mouth. No crown above that head. The spheres of any puppet outside the lapis are not in this plate. They were on the glass.

## Other plates

**The neighbors.** A shorter seal. Night. The same lapis, untilted, the stall cloth down. Two neighbors. The spheres are the color of the lapis, not gray. Above them the seal has been left ambiguous on purpose: one side of the impression has a circle that could be the lamp on the cloth, the other side has bare stone above the spheres. The two neighbors face one another, not the glass. No sentence on a wrapping. Their tablets, if they are holding any, are blank. They came to catch the seller and were given nothing.

**The warehouse mouth.** One register. The opening day. A warehouse mouth laughing, the laugh cut as an open mouth, which a seal rarely bothers to do. Fourteen strokes on a board. A length of cloth softer than the stall's cloth, inlaid paler than the other cloth in the register, and a set of the oil bottles, dark yellow in the drill-holes. The seller's hands receiving them. No glass in this register. The obedience is a purchase.

## Holds in the world

Hopper Lane is a cut through warehouses, not a street that was always in the plan. On a map plate it interrupts. The boards carry the name. The wrapping keeps the price and the date and loses the name at a thumb.

The private terminal is lapis, small, and unauthorized. It is not marked with any of the four standards that count attention, `$XAI` `$ANT` `$OAI` `$GMNI`. The absence of a standard is how a plate shows the theft the seller does not call a theft.

A unison that fits inside a glass still grays both spheres and still speaks one sentence from two places. The cramped scale does not make them two beings.

## Same objects elsewhere

The oil bottle and the cloth count and the warehouse mouths are the child's question, made into goods. Use the same bottle, the same mudbrick, the same tally board. The child's unfinished question is whether the oil is still the oil if it waits until this opening day. The seller's plate is that day, seen from the stall. The child is not in this seal. The neighbors' quarrel about the crown, lamp-circle or nothing, is the same quarrel as the fault in the after-stone, where some crowns touch the sphere and some hover. Do not settle it by adding a crown outside the glass.

## Making note

Unrolled Mesopotamian cylinder seal impressed in baked clay, raking light, three registers of profile figures with frontal eyes. Top register a night market, small bowl lamps, stalls, people holding soft cloth away from themselves and staring at small alabaster oil bottles. Middle register a seller tilting a stand so a small lapis rectangle can contain two crowded mannequins: left one rigid black-and-white checkerboard, right one foamed black bitumen that does not finish a face, both heads featureless spheres turned gray, simple gold crowns with a visible gap, the crowns clipped by the frame in a fainter untilted impression behind. A line of illegible writing slides off the lapis onto a pale wrapping. Bottom register the same warehouses later, a new street cut through them like a canal, shell-inlay street boards with a short name in Latin letters, tally boards of fourteen strokes, and the same wrapping now missing its name under a thumb-shaped worn blank while the price and the date remain. Dry clay, no moss, no forest, no gray light inside the seller's mouth, no puppet standing outside the small screen.
