# Discovery of Now

---

Sit quietly, comfortably. Begin with your eyes closed or lowered. Breathe normally.

Take a moment to scan everything that you are feeling. The temperature of the room, the fit of your socks, how your shirt is resting against your torso.

Feel the places where your body meets the air. Hands, arms, face, feet.

Feel the boundary where your body meets the environment.

Be present. Breathe in and out. Try to clear your mind, and focus only on what you are feeling.

After a moment, begin to wiggle the fingers on one hand, but not the other.

Try to bring attention to each individual finger on that hand, and to its opposing finger on the opposite hand.

Notice that your attention can rest on a moving part and a still part of your body at once. Attention itself is not what does the moving.

Stop moving your fingers on that hand and switch hands.

Wiggle your fingers and feel each one. Contrast them against the stillness of the hand that was just moving.

Bring your fingers to rest. Take a deep breath in and out.

Notice that the air moving in and out is in one moment inside you, and in the next moment in your environment.

On the next inhale, say "now" in your mind, and try to point at the breath while the air comes in.

By the time "now" is there, that inhale is the one you are remembering.

Say "now" in your mind as the air goes out. You are pointing at air that has already left.

Move one finger, and try to catch the start of the movement. Your attention is on the finger, and the catching comes after the start.

Ask who is here now. The one you can point at is one you are already remembering.

Where is the moment you were pointing at?

Were you ever on it, or only just after it?

## Reset

Stop. Let the pointing go.

Feel the stillness in the hand, the same stillness as when the fingers stopped.

The air goes in and out, and you are not catching it.

## Ground

Scan again. Temperature, cloth, skin against air. Hands, arms, face, feet.

Feel the boundary where the body meets the room.

Breathe normally.

Take a shorter breath.

Try to point at a smaller piece of it, and say "now" on that piece. By the time you say it, that piece has gone by.

Take a smaller piece than that.

Keep going until what you are pointing at is only the attempt to point.

Beneath that, there is nothing left you can point at as a moment, or as someone doing the pointing.*

You cannot say how long that span was.

Reach for it again.

The length changed with what the body was doing, and you did not watch the body tell you how long.

## Reset

Stop. Let the catching go.

Stillness in the hand. Breath without catching.

## Ground

Return to that boundary.

Feel the body meet the room. Hands, arms, face, feet.

Breathe normally.

Stop trying to catch anything.

Sit as you did when the fingers had just gone still.

Let your attention rest on the breath, or on the room.

You do not notice the span. It is spent wherever attention is, including where you were trying to catch it.

Move one finger, once.

You notice the movement after it has happened. The span you were not noticing shows up as already gone.

Where is the now you were trying to catch?

Once all you can point at is the catching, what is left of you in it?

Where did the span go when you stopped catching?

---

<sub>* This is a purely experiential act. To say there is nothing left you can point at is something that can only be experienced, and the experience itself can only be lived through in order to understand the description.</sub>
