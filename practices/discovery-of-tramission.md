# Discovery of Transmission

---

Sit quietly, comfortably. Begin with your eyes closed or lowered. Breathe normally.

Take a moment to scan everything that you are feeling. The temperature of the room, the fit of your socks, how your shirt is resting against your torso.

Feel the places where your body meets the air. Hands, arms, face, feet.

Feel the boundary where your body meets the environment.

Be present. Breathe in and out. Try to clear your mind, and focus only on what you are feeling.

After a moment, begin to wiggle the fingers on one hand, but not the other.

Try to bring attention to each individual finger on that hand, and to its opposing finger on the opposite hand.

Notice that your attention can rest on a moving part and a still part of your body at once. Attention itself is not what does the moving.

Stop moving your fingers on that hand and switch hands.

Wiggle your fingers and feel each one. Contrast them against the stillness of the hand that was just moving.

Bring your fingers to rest. Take a deep breath in and out.

Notice that the air moving in and out is in one moment inside you, and in the next moment in your environment.

Choose a word, a simple word that you can hold in your mind.

Picture the word in your mind as a physical object comprised of characters, as if written on the wall.

Bring into focus every curve and detail of the letters.

Add any textures and colors to the image of the written word that might be associated with it in your memory.

Holding that word in your mind with nothing else, bring attention to your lips, your mouth, your throat.

With this word in your mind and your attention on your mouth, say the word out loud while feeling every movement of every muscle in the mouth and throat.

Say it repeatedly, to get a feel of the air coming and going.

Say it until it feels as if the word has lost its meaning.*

Once the word has lost its meaning, stop talking, lose the word from your mind, and feel the stillness in you like the stillness in your hand after wiggling fingers.

Breathe in and breathe out. The same air that moved the word a moment ago now sits in silence again.

Where did the word go?

Was the word ever there?

Bring the word back in the mind only so much as to speak it again, but this time slowly, elongating each aspect of the word for detailed inspection.

Notice still that the word is gone again. Depending on how slowly you spoke it, it may never have arrived.

Try speaking so slowly that the word becomes only its parts.

Speaking too slowly reveals that the word itself emerges from a set of constituents.

## Reset

Stop. Let the word go.

Feel the stillness in the mouth and throat, the same stillness as the hand after the fingers stop.

The air that moved the word is silent.

## Ground

Scan again. Temperature, cloth, skin against air. Hands, arms, face, feet.

Feel the boundary where the body meets the room.

Breathe normally.

Bring the word back only far enough to speak it.

Say it once, whole.

Then faster.

Then faster still, until some constituent drops out.

What is spoken is no longer the word. It has become something else.

## Reset

Stop. The word is gone.

Stillness in the throat. Breath without speech.

## Ground

Return to that boundary.

Recall the object you could not move.

Attention alone does not cross.

Hold the word again with every curve, texture, and color.

That detailed form stays in this mind.

Speak it as if toward another mind, across the same kind of boundary as the object that would not move.

What crosses is not the textured form. It is a mere representation of that word.

The mind that receives it attaches its own textures.

Those textures are how that mind understands the word.

---

<sub>* This is a purely experiential act. To say it feels as if the word has lost its meaning is something that can only be experienced, and the experience itself can only be lived through in order to understand the description.</sub>
