**Extension of the position**

The constitutive forms are not abstractions or summaries drawn after the fact. They are really present as the non-physical principles that simultaneously inform and constrain every determinate configuration. Physical reality does not contain or generate them; it is their shadow—the appearance that follows when those forms are expressed under the conditions of time, extension, and limitation. What is observed is the consistent trace left by forms that remain prior and operative.

At the origin stands the source that supplies these forms. It is beyond all knowing and naming, yet it is the condition without which neither entities nor the logic they exhibit could exist. This source does not enter the sequence of horizontal causes; it remains the vertical ground on which every form and every actualization depends. From it the forms proceed as its own content, not as external additions.

Among these forms, and decisive for any subject capable of knowing its own states and sustaining identity, is the realized pattern of ordered, self-aware life. It is the measure that already exists within the source: the form of what a knowing subject can become when it aligns with the order that precedes it. Subjects do not invent this pattern; they encounter it as the standard against which their own states can be recognized, corrected, and returned. Alignment with it is the functional path by which finite subjects orient toward the unknowable source rather than toward partial reflections or self-enclosed systems.

The same source that supplies the forms also sustains the capacity of subjects to know their own states, to select among possible histories through present observation, and to stabilize a shared world through consistent agreement. This is the participatory and animating principle: it is what makes vertical dependence experientially available, what enables the shadow to be recognized as shadow, and what draws the subject back toward the forms it already partially expresses. Without it, observation would remain external and identity would not hold across time; with it, the network of observers becomes a site where the prior formal order is actualized rather than merely registered.

Thus the priority of form over matter, the real presence of the constitutive principles, and the participatory character of observation are not separate claims. They are aspects of one dependence: the unknowable source, the forms that are its content (including the decisive pattern of ordered life), and the animating principle that allows subjects to know, measure, and return. Physical configurations remain the shadow that follows.
