**Summary of beliefs**

Constitutive forms are non-physical principles prior to any physical configuration. They inform and constrain what can exist and how it can be structured. Physical things express this order; they do not generate it. Absolute nothing supplies neither entities nor logic, so the source of the logic reality exhibits must already be operative at the first determinate existence.

Dependence is both horizontal (temporal sequences of causes) and vertical (simultaneous hierarchical determination by which form specifies what matter can be). Consciousness involves a subject that can know its own states and sustain identity across time; it is not a late statistical byproduct. Observers participate in actualization, and shared observation stabilizes a common world. In this sense the formal and participatory order is primary; matter is its appearance.

Jesus is fully man and fully God. His physical body is the expression of a structure intended in the Word from the beginning. The incarnation belongs to that prior intention and would have occurred even without the fall (though without the sacrifice). The baptism is the moment the fullness of divinity is again manifest in the man, grace reconnected and the anointing received.

Adam and Eve represent an earlier mode of the human, prior to humanity as it is now known. The capacity of the tree of knowledge was appointed for them, yet they were not mature enough to receive it. Discernment arrived without its accompanying understanding. They registered nakedness before they grasped what nakedness meant; the resulting shame marks that mismatch. Original sin is the ongoing consequence of this separation.

Language is the medium by which one mind communicates with another. No mind reaches another directly; the body must encode a link (speech, gesture, mark) that the receiving mind reconstructs. Communication is learned before critical thought, so the assumptions carried by language—about the world, about selves, about what counts as real—are structurally embedded in the developing person through repeated engagement with other beings. These assumptions arrive as social remnants: not only stories, but the unspoken patterns of conduct and the arrangements already producing behavior when a new mind arrives. They impress the way habits are imprinted, and they shape how later remnants and records are taken up.

Because the medium itself is formed under the pressure of group coordination and carries the prior history of shared forms (externalized into objects, ritual sequences, and compressed accounts so that shapes can persist beyond any single speaker), it transmits discernment and relation before the corresponding understanding is present. The taint of sin, on this account, resides in that medium: the gap between minds is crossed by a language that already embeds the mismatch of premature discernment, so that what passes from mind to mind carries the distortion. The hardened record of what is actually done remains the more reliable check, but the instrument that reads it is itself conditioned by the same communicative history.
