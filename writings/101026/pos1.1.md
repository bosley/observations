**A position**

1. Consciousness is not a late statistical summary of non-conscious processes. It involves a subject that can know its own states and sustain identity across time.

2. Absolute nothing supplies neither entities nor logic. Therefore the source of the logic that physical reality exhibits must be available at least as early as the first determinate existence. This source remains beyond all knowing and naming; it is the vertical ground, not one more entity within the order it makes possible.

3. Two modes of dependence are distinguished. Horizontal causality is the temporal sequence of efficient causes. Vertical causality is the simultaneous hierarchical dependence by which form determines what matter can be.

4. Constitutive forms exist independently of any particular mind and are really present as non-physical principles. They inform and constrain physical reality; they are known only through the regularities and limits they impose. Physical configurations do not contain or generate them.

5. At the first discernible now, a principle capable of supplying determinate forms must already be operative. Physical configurations do not generate the formal order they display; they express it. What appears is the shadow that follows when those forms are rendered under the conditions of time, extension, and limitation.

6. Observers participate in the actualization of reality. Present observation can select among possible histories, and networks of observers stabilize a shared world through consistent measurement and agreement. This participation is sustained by the same source that supplies the forms: it is the animating principle that makes self-knowledge, identity across time, and the recognition of the shadow as shadow possible.

7. Mind, in the broad sense of the formal and participatory order, is not secondary to matter. Matter is the appearance or effect of that order. In this abstracted sense, all is mind.

8. Among the forms that proceed as the content of the source, and decisive for any subject that knows its own states, is the realized pattern of ordered, self-aware life. It is the measure already present within the source. Subjects do not invent it; they encounter it as the standard against which their states can be recognized and aligned. Orientation through this pattern is orientation toward the source rather than toward partial reflections or self-enclosed systems.
