**A position**

1. Consciousness is not a late statistical summary of non-conscious processes. It involves a subject that can know its own states and sustain identity across time.

2. Absolute nothing supplies neither entities nor logic. Therefore the source of the logic that physical reality exhibits must be available at least as early as the first determinate existence.

3. Two modes of dependence are distinguished. Horizontal causality is the temporal sequence of efficient causes. Vertical causality is the simultaneous hierarchical dependence by which form determines what matter can be.

4. Constitutive forms exist independently of any particular mind. They are non-physical principles that inform and constrain physical reality. They are known only through the regularities and limits they impose.

5. At the first discernible now, a principle capable of supplying determinate forms must already be operative. Physical configurations do not generate the formal order they display; they express it.

6. Observers participate in the actualization of reality. Present observation can select among possible histories, and networks of observers stabilize a shared world through consistent measurement and agreement.

7. Mind, in the broad sense of the formal and participatory order, is not secondary to matter. Matter is the appearance or effect of that order. In this abstracted sense, all is mind.
