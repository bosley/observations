**Creedal boundary**  
Jesus is fully man and fully God, in the sense professed by the LCMS creeds.

**Christ and the baptism**  
From the primordiant perspective: after his baptism he re-filled his true divinity, having reconnected to grace and been anointed.  
Christ’s physical body was crafted from the structure of the Word (reality) that was intended since the dawn of time.  
The Christ would have appeared even if the fall had never happened; in that case he would not have been sacrificed.

**Adam and Eve**  
Adam and Eve are the story of the beings that were human before human beings were as we know them today.  
The fruit of the tree of knowledge was meant for them, but they were immature and not yet ready.

**Original sin**  
Original sin is a ramification of a function that permitted discernment before understanding.  
Understanding was meant to arrive in tandem with discernment.  
They became aware of being naked before they understood what it was to be naked; that mismatch is the source of the shame.

This is the position as given. It keeps the full divinity and full humanity of Christ, locates the body of Christ in a prior formal structure of the Word, treats the incarnation as intended independently of the fall (with the cross as contingent on the fall), and re-reads the Genesis narrative as an account of immature proto-humans who received a capacity for discernment without the corresponding understanding.

**A coherent position**

Reality is ordered by constitutive principles that are prior to any physical configuration. These principles are non-physical; they determine what can exist, how it can be structured, and what regularities it must exhibit. Physical things do not generate this order; they express it. The order is therefore available at least as early as the first determinate state of affairs, because that state already displays intelligible form. Absolute absence supplies neither entities nor the logic those entities follow; the source of the logic must already be operative when anything determinate appears.

Dependence runs in two registers. One is the ordinary sequence of causes through time. The other is the simultaneous hierarchical relation by which form specifies what matter can be. The second register is what makes any temporal sequence intelligible as this kind of sequence rather than sheer succession.

Consciousness is not a late statistical byproduct of non-conscious processes. It involves a subject capable of knowing its own states and of maintaining identity across time. Observers participate in the actualization of what is observed: present attention can select among possible histories, and shared attention stabilizes a common world. In this sense the formal and participatory order is not secondary to matter; matter is the appearance that order takes. Abstractly, all is mind.

Within this order the body of Christ is the physical expression of a structure intended in the Word from the beginning. The incarnation belongs to that prior intention; it is not an afterthought required only by the fall. Had there been no fall, the Christ would still have appeared, though without the sacrifice. The baptism marks the moment in which the fullness of divinity is again manifest in the man, grace reconnected and the anointing received.

Adam and Eve stand for an earlier mode of the human, prior to humanity as it is now known. The capacity represented by the tree of knowledge was appointed for them, yet they were not mature enough to receive it. What followed was discernment arriving without its accompanying understanding. They registered their nakedness before they grasped what nakedness meant; the resulting shame is the mark of that mismatch. Original sin is the ongoing consequence of a function that separated the two.

The same formal priority that structures physical reality therefore structures the scriptural narrative: the Word’s intention precedes and informs both the body of Christ and the unfinished humanity of the first pair.
