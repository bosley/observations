# Reflection of selfs

The outer self is what you did. Actions, attributes, fruits. Things a stranger could know without being told why you did any of it.

The inner self is what you feel while you look at that. You don't explain the act to yourself and then feel whatever the explanation leaves you with. You feel it as if you had no idea why the person did it.

The higher self is the right hand. Selfless acts, and the fruits. The lower self is the left hand. Selfish acts, including the ones you're told are evil and feel nothing about. You contemplate the higher and the lower by feeling the inner, and the inner only gets the outer. If a reason for the act shows up, you're back inside the person, and the stranger is gone.

1. Sit still, comfortable. Not rigid, and not slumped. You want a seat you can stay in without adjusting every few breaths.

   Breathe in deep until you can't anymore. Hold it a couple seconds. Let it out really slowly, and do it again. The hold is short. The out-breath is the long part.

   On the in-breath, visualize light coming in with the air. On the out-breath, visualize darkness, or shadow, going out with it. Each breath, both. The light comes in. The shadow leaves. You aren't deciding what either of them means. Its just "good in," "bad out," and whatever your mind takes it to mean, it means.

   Stay on the breath. Not on a count, and not on a word. The way the air feels as it goes into the lungs, what temperature it is, and the light and the shadow moving with it. Cooler on the way in, warmer on the way out, or whatever it actually is where you're sitting.

2. Hold no thought. This is not a fight to keep the mind empty. Let the thoughts come if they come. You don't chase them and you don't answer them.

   If you get distracted, forgive yourself, purge them, and continue. The distraction already happened. Forgiving it is so you don't spend the next breath on the fact that you got distracted. Purge means you drop it and go back to the air. You don't examine what the thought was.

   Whenever the mind wanders, go back to the light and the dark on the breath. In on the light, out on the shadow. It gives the mind a place to sit until it settles. Use it any time later in the sitting, not only here.

3. Keep this for one to three minutes. You don't have to get all the way quiet before you move on. A clean stretch is not what lets you take the next step. It's setting the tongue. You're putting the mind where it's going to work from, the way you set the tongue before you speak.

   With enough practice the mind will go quiet. That comes from doing this often. It is not what you're waiting on. Keep the one to three minutes anyway, no matter how still you already are. If you went quiet in thirty seconds, you still finish the minute. If you never went quiet, you still move on when the time is up.

4. When you're ready, consider the self. Ready means the tongue is set, not that the mind is blank. You're listing what a stranger could already see.

   - Self, remember. Who are you, and how did you get here. Not your name and not your job title. What you've done up to now, and the order you did it in. If the answer starts explaining why, drop it and ask again. Why stays inside. It doesn't go in the list.

   - What attributes do you possess, and which ones you don't. Both, and only the ones someone else could see. An attribute you feel you have, with nothing outside to show it, is the inner talking. Leave it off. An attribute you don't have is not a failure. It's just one you don't have.

   - What actions you partake in that are purely for selfish reasons. Look at it from the outside. Who did the act serve, if you don't get to say. If it only looks selfish, or only stops looking selfish, once you explain yourself, leave it out. Those go in the left hand. That's the lower self, as far as anyone watching could see it. You don't weigh them yet. You place them.

   - Clear the mind. Same as before. Forgive, purge, back to nothing. The left hand is full. You don't carry those acts into the next question or they'll stain it.

   - Self, remember. What actions you partake in that are purely for selfless reasons. Same look, from the outside. If it only looks selfless once you supply why you did it, it isn't in this hand. This hand is the higher self. It only gets what someone else could see without your help.

   - What are your fruits. What came of the acts, not what you meant to come of them. A stranger can see fruit. A stranger can't see what you meant. Those go in the right hand, with the selfless acts. Any face/mask you take, and any fruit you make that someone else can observe. 

5. Sit with your back straight. You've been comfortable. Now you sit up, because the hands are about to do the remembering for you.

   Bring the hands in front of you. Both hands make a fist, resting comfortably. Closed, not clenched. The left fist is the lower self. The right fist is the higher self. You are not looking at either of them yet. They're just sitting there. Don't start feeling into them.

6. Open the right hand and contemplate the higher self. Look at what's actually in it. The acts, and the fruits. Nothing you remember feeling while you did them.

   Don't boast. Boasting is you climbing back in to add credit nobody else would have given you. If you catch yourself telling the story of how good it was, or why it was hard, close the hand, purge, and open it again.

   Recognize yourself as a stranger. This is the whole thing, not a mood you try to hold. You're looking at this hand the way you'd look at someone you don't know the reasons of. All you have is what they did and what came of it. Someone else's good is just good. It doesn't get a speech. See it, and notice what you feel looking at it. That feeling is the inner self. It doesn't get to change what's in the hand.

7. Close the right hand. The good goes back in the fist. It stays. You are not done with it. You are only done looking.

   Empty the mind. If you carry the right hand into the left, you'll use the good to soften what's coming. That's what this step is there to stop.

8. Open the left hand and contemplate the lower self. The selfish acts. The things you know are evil, and the things you're told are evil but feel no remorse for. Both. Still as a stranger. You don't get the reason you had at the time. The second kind is where you usually break in, because you don't feel bad and the explanation offers to fill that in.

   Don't excuse yourself. No context, no "but they," no "it was a long time ago," no "what I meant was." If a reason shows up, that's you trying to get back into a hand that doesn't take reasons. Drop it and look at the act.

   Feel the guilt. The inner self does the feeling, and all it has is what you did. Not a show of guilt, and not a verdict you say out loud. The feeling, for as long as the hand is open. If there's no remorse for a thing you were told is evil, don't invent some so it looks like you're taking it seriously. Notice that there's none. You see that too.

9. Close the left hand. The selfish acts go back in the fist. You don't leave the hand open to keep punishing yourself, and you don't shake them out. They're sitting there again, same as the right.

10. Cycle the left and the right. Open one, contemplate, close it, empty the mind, open the other. The empty mind between them matters as much as either hand. Without it the two sides start arguing, and then you're just thinking.

    Keep going until you can't take anymore, or until it's hard to keep focus on the good. Those are the two stops. The first is that the guilt, or the cycling itself, has worn you out. The second is quieter. The right hand stops being visible. You open it and the good won't hold still, or it comes out thin next to the left. That's when you stop. Don't push past it to prove you can still see the good.

    Then sit still, mind blank, forgive yourself. The forgiveness here is the same one from the start. You did the thing you sat down to do. You're not putting a judgment on top of it. The hands stay closed. This is the end of the reflection.

## Merging of self

Extended. Only after the reflection of selfs. Don't open both hands in the same sitting where you first put the acts in them. The reflection has to be something you've already done, on its own, before you mix anything.

1. Sit the way you did for the reflection. Breath, light in, shadow out, one to three minutes, tongue set. If the mind wanders, back to the breath.

2. Bring the hands up in fists, the same way. Left is the lower self, right is the higher self, from the reflection you already did. You are not filling them again. If you can't remember what was in a hand without rebuilding it, you aren't ready for this sitting. Go back and do the reflection.

3. Open both hands and breathe deep. This is the first time both are open together. Let them mix. You are not comparing them and you are not weighing which hand is heavier.

    Whatever was most prevalent in the left hand, have it meet the right hand. Not the whole list. The thing that kept being there when the left hand was open.

    Not to justify the evil. Not to boast in the good. Not to excuse the evil. If it turns into any of those three, you've left the step. Close both hands, empty the mind, and open them again.

    To see the whole picture. Higher and lower together, and you don't put the reasons back in. In a sterile environment, the pure self as you see it in this moment. Sterile means the stranger is still the one looking. You aren't warming it up and you aren't going after it. The inner self feels whatever it feels about all of it. It still doesn't get to explain the person. The pure self here is not a better self. It's this self, seen from the outside, with none of the why left in it.

4. Hold that image and scan it. Don't grab the first thing that shows up and call it the answer. Move across the picture. What comes to mind, and what sits at the highest, those are the focus points.

    Highest doesn't mean worst, and it doesn't mean most impressive. It means the thing in the picture that the rest of it hangs off of. There may be more than one.

    Those are the things to bring balance to. A focus point is where the higher and the lower, seen from the outside, don't meet. Not the spot where your own explanation of yourself is softest. The inner felt it. It doesn't get to pick a different spot because it knows why.

5. If you feel balance, continue to do the work. Balance isn't a feeling that the sitting went well. It's that the picture held still and the focus points were visible. The work is whatever those points ask for, done after you get up, with the hands empty again. If you don't feel balance, you don't invent it. You just stop. The sitting did what it did.

6. See yourself in the future, if you were able to put away all the negative you've been wrestling with. Not a wish. A picture of that person, the way they stand once that weight is off them.

    The idea comes from solving 2d mazes. You can solve the maze by looking at the end and tracing back to the beginning. Same here. You look at the end first.

7. See that person the way you'd remember a past self. Fully aware of who was sitting here, and who is sitting here. You are looking across time at yourself.

    Come in from far to near. Five years from now. Then four. Then three. Then two. Then one. Then tomorrow. Then not far at all, just a moment before. "I" was there. Forgive yourself, and be better for it.

8. Meet yourself in line with the next step. The ideal self, the true self, is the one at the far end, in the vision he exists in. You don't jump to him. You see what you look like in the goal, then trace back until the next step is the one that lines up with where you're sitting. That's the step you take. Bringing yourself onto the path to the better you.
