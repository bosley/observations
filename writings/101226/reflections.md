# Reflection of selfs

The outer self is the record. Actions, attributes, fruits. Things a stranger could know if they only had the record, with no access to why you did any of it.

The inner self is what you feel while you look at that record. You don't get to brief it. You don't explain the act to yourself and then feel whatever the explanation earns. The feeling comes off the record as if you had no internal understanding of the person who did it.

The higher self is the right hand. Selfless acts, and the fruits. The lower self is the left hand. Selfish acts, including the ones you're told are evil and feel nothing about. You contemplate the higher and the lower by feeling the inner, and the inner only gets the outer. Motive stays out. If a motive gets in, you're back inside the person, and the stranger is gone.

1. Sit still, comfortable. Not rigid, and not slumped. You want a seat you can stay in without adjusting every few breaths.

   Breathe in deep until you can't anymore. Hold it a couple seconds. Let it out really slowly, and do it again. The hold is short. The out-breath is the long part.

   On the in-breath, visualize light coming in with the air. On the out-breath, visualize darkness, or shadow, going out with it. Each breath, both. The light comes in. The shadow leaves. You aren't deciding what either of them means yet. The hands do that later. Right now it's just the picture that rides the air.

   Stay on the breath. Not on a count, and not on a word. The way the air feels as it goes into the lungs, what temperature it is, and the light and the shadow moving with it. Cooler on the way in, warmer on the way out, or whatever it actually is where you're sitting.

2. Hold no thought. This is not a fight to keep the mind empty. Let the thoughts come if they come. You don't chase them and you don't answer them.

   If you get distracted, forgive yourself, purge them, and continue. The distraction already happened. Forgiving it is so you don't spend the next breath on the fact that you got distracted. Purge means you drop it and go back to the air. You don't examine what the thought was.

3. Keep this for one to three minutes. The point is not that it go completely uninterrupted before you move on. A clean stretch is not the requirement for the next step. It's setting the tongue. You're putting the mind in the posture it's going to work from, the way you set the tongue before you speak.

   With enough practice the mind will go quiet. That quiet is a side effect of doing this often. It is not the gate. Keep the one to three minutes anyway, no matter how still you already are. If you went quiet in thirty seconds, you still finish the minute. If you never went quiet, you still move on when the time is up.

4. When you're ready, consider the self. Ready here means the tongue is set, not that the mind is blank. You're building the record a stranger would have. Not the account you'd give a friend.

   - Self, remember. Who are you, and how did you get here. Not your name and not your job title. What the record shows up to now, and the sequence of acts that got it here. If the answer starts explaining why, drop it and ask again. Why is internal. It doesn't go on the record.

   - What attributes do you possess, and which ones you don't. Both, and only the ones the record can support. An attribute you feel you have, with nothing outside to show it, is the inner talking. Leave it off. An attribute you don't possess is not a failure. It's a gap in the record.

   - What actions you partake in that are purely for selfish reasons. Judge it from the outside. Who did the act serve, if you don't get to say. If it only holds up once you explain yourself, it isn't pure, and it doesn't go here. Those go in the left hand. That's the lower self, as far as the record can show it. You don't weigh them yet. You place them.

   - Clear the mind. Same as before. Forgive, purge, back to nothing. The left hand is full. You don't carry those acts into the next question or they'll stain it.

   - Self, remember. What actions you partake in that are purely for selfless reasons. Same test, from the outside. If it only looks selfless once you supply the motive, it isn't in this hand. This hand is the higher self, and it only gets what the record can carry without your help.

   - What are your fruits. What came of the acts, not what you meant to come of them. A stranger can see fruit. A stranger can't see an intention. Those go in the right hand, with the selfless acts. Fruit you can't point at doesn't go in the hand.

5. Sit with your back straight. You've been comfortable. Now the posture changes, because the hands are about to do the remembering for you.

   Bring the hands in front of you. Both hands make a fist, resting comfortably. Closed, not clenched. The left fist is the lower self. The right fist is the higher self. You are not looking at either of them yet. They're just seated, and the inner hasn't been let at them.

6. Open the right hand and contemplate the higher self. Look at what's actually in it. The acts, and the fruits. Nothing you remember feeling while you did them.

   Don't boast. Boasting is the inner climbing back in to add credit the record doesn't have. If you catch yourself narrating how good it was, or why it was hard, close the hand, purge, and open it again.

   Recognize yourself as a stranger. This is the whole method, not a mood. You're looking at this hand the way you'd look at someone you have no internal understanding of. All you have is what they did and what came of it. A stranger's good is just good. It doesn't get a speech. See it, and notice what you feel looking at it. That feeling is the inner self. It doesn't get to edit the hand.

7. Close the right hand. The good goes back in the fist. It stays. You are not done with it. You are only done looking.

   Empty the mind. If you carry the right hand into the left, you'll use the good to soften what's coming. That's the thing this step is there to stop.

8. Open the left hand and contemplate the lower self. The selfish acts. The things you know are evil, and the things you're told are evil but feel no remorse for. Both. Still as a stranger. You don't get the reason you had at the time. The second kind is where the inner usually breaks in, because the remorse isn't there and the explanation offers to fill the gap.

   Don't excuse yourself. No context, no "but they," no "it was a long time ago," no "what I meant was." If a reason shows up, that's internal understanding trying to get back into a hand that isn't allowed to have any. Drop it and look at the act.

   Feel the guilt. The inner self is what does the feeling, and all it has to go on is the outer record. Not the performance of guilt, and not a verdict you announce. The feeling, for as long as the hand is open. If there's no remorse for a thing you were told is evil, don't invent some so the picture looks serious. Notice that there's none. That absence is part of what the inner sees.

9. Close the left hand. The selfish acts go back in the fist. You don't leave the hand open to keep punishing yourself, and you don't shake them out. They're seated again, same as the right.

10. Cycle the left and the right. Open one, contemplate, close it, empty the mind, open the other. The empty mind between them matters as much as either hand. Without it the two sides start arguing, and then you're just thinking.

    Keep going until you can't take anymore, or until it's hard to keep focus on the good. Those are the two stops. The first is that the guilt, or the cycling itself, has used you up. The second is subtler. The right hand stops being visible. You open it and the good won't hold still, or it comes out thin next to the left. That's the signal. Don't push past it to prove you can still see the good.

    Then sit still, mind blank, forgive yourself, and be ready to move on. The forgiveness here is the same one from the start. You did the thing you sat down to do. You're not adding a judgment on top of it.

11. Open both hands and breathe deep. This is the first time both are open together. Let them mix. You are not comparing them and you are not weighing which hand is heavier.

    Whatever was most prevalent in the left hand, have it meet the right hand. Not the whole list. The thing that kept being there when the left hand was open.

    Not to justify the evil. Not to boast in the good. Not to excuse the evil. If the meeting turns into any of those three, you've left the step. Close both hands, empty the mind, and open them again.

    To see the whole picture. Higher and lower, one record, no motive added back in. And in a sterile environment, the pure self as you see it in this moment. Sterile means the stranger is still the one looking. You aren't warming it up and you aren't prosecuting it. The inner self feels whatever it feels about the whole record. It still doesn't get to explain the person. The pure self here is not a better self. It's this self, seen from the outside, with no internal understanding left in the picture.

12. Hold that image and scan it. Don't grab the first thing that appears and call it the answer. Move across the picture. What comes to mind, and what sits at the highest, those are the focus points.

    Highest doesn't mean worst, and it doesn't mean most impressive. It means the thing in the picture that the rest of the picture hangs off of. There may be more than one.

    Those are the things to bring balance to. A focus point is where the higher and the lower, read from the outside, don't meet. Not where your explanation of yourself is most tender. The inner felt it. The inner doesn't get to pick a different spot because it knows the backstory.

13. If you feel balance, continue to do the work. Balance isn't a feeling that the sitting went well. It's that the picture held still and the focus points were visible. The work is whatever those points ask for, done after you get up, with the hands empty again. If you don't feel balance, you don't invent it. You just stop. The sitting did what it did.
