# Something rather than nothing

Contemplating something versus nothing, I assume something can come from nothing. From nothing, nothing comes. That can't be proven. True nothing has no attributes, and a law would be an attribute. It is dimensionless. I am a thing, and anything I could imagine is a thing, so I can't begin to conceive true nothing. A darkness beyond evil, not even indifference, a void. You shouldn't think about that for too long. Very similar to contemplating various aspects of infinity, and it seems to drive people a little nuts.

A prohibition needs an everything before it can apply. True nothing is a passive concept. The everything is more of a masculine concept, an assertion of law and of form, because if everything is going to exist it has to exist a certain way, or it could destroy itself. I guess I can't really say how a totality would be expressed. That is as incomprehensible as the nothing.

What I take as having come is everything. In my own work I call it the Primordiant, a labeled boundary of human conception. People who stay deep in prayer a very long time, the things saints do, seem to be approaching this all-thing, the opposite of true nothing. The mind changes in a similar way whichever story they hold to be true, and the ramifications are wildly different.

If there was true nothing and then suddenly all of everything, "suddenly" already assumes a before. Time itself would be contained inside the all-thing. Our cosmology seems to suggest a now. I don't trust any scientific model with respect to this yet. It would be inappropriate cross-layering of sorts. It is interesting that the Big Bang seems to show this. The very first second, the very first now, was able to be a thing out of the all-thing, which implies there were things before time itself, and not nothing. That's the realm of Plato's forms. You can't say eventually of the whole. Time was set almost as if begotten rather than made.

If time and space as a substance came on as a downstream byproduct of everything emerging in the face of nothing, and the forms which precede the arrangements of matter are part of the all-thing, then those forms should show up as patterns in nature. Which is interesting, because we figured out the forms were a thing by looking at nature. Geometry, and algebra off of geometry. Patterns that follow laws and rules. Where do the laws come from.

At the highest energies right after the Big Bang, the forces we know were not yet distinct. Around 10^-43 seconds there is speculation of a single force including gravity. Gravity is thought to have separated first. From about 10^-36 to 10^-35 seconds, the strong, the weak, and the electromagnetic are theorized as one grand unified force. As the universe expanded and cooled, the symmetry broke, like water freezing. The strong force separated. Later, around 10^-12 to 10^-10 seconds, the electroweak force, which has been experimentally confirmed unified at high energies, broke when the Higgs field settled to a non-zero vacuum expectation value, particles got mass, and electromagnetism and the weak force split. The energy is conserved and redistributed. The unified state differentiated into the backdrop that allowed matter to arrange, the first stars, later stars, the elements, and us here today.

It seems to me that nature rhymes. As above, so below. At the first now there is a set of forms, the unified everything that could be formed under some imposed rule set of the all, and time emerges from that, and then the unified force is played out as the forces we recognize.

The all-thing, existing, has nothing for a contrast, so a nothing gets conceived. A conceived nothing is a representation of nothing, on the side of something. Then a conception of that conception, ad infinitum. That's at least how I comprehend it, as a programmer. The inclination to use some other word than nothing, for true nothing, is where the secret orders came up with plug-and-play gods for their neophytes. Thelema has Nuit, infinite space, the night sky of pure potential, "Infinite Space and the Infinite Stars thereof," and Hadit, the infinitely small point, the self, the motion and experience at the center of every star. The layers are the veils of negative existence before the Tree of Life, Ain, Ain Soph, Ain Soph Aur, concentrating into Kether. To me that is a recursive self-analyzation of everything against nothing, and it might be a slight miss on the analogy. They tend to express a divine union. I see it as an observation of the opposite, then an awareness of that awareness.

If the sun just disappeared, everyone would say oh, it's nighttime. The sun is gone, and what's left is darkness. Night means a sun on the other side of the world. Darkness is similar to night and existentially different, and it has attributes, where the void has none.

Or no. I can't say the all-thing notices. It appears to me as the whole thing, by existing in the face of nothing, defining itself, and emerging as everything.

I'm not going to say any of these stories is true or false. They overlay this, or they don't.

If no attribute can be said of God, then God hasn't been described. The logos is the word spoken, and I can take the all-thing as the one speaking, and the speech as the forms, or the laws the forms follow. That would later inform the makeup of reality, and later the beings of this planet, perhaps for those beings. The word as a persona, the collection of attributes of the word that we can understand, made flesh in God's only son, Jesus Christ.

The Greek tales have chaos, the gods after that, and men crafted by the gods, and the personhood and the morals are obviously different.

The Valentinians take the forms, a Pleroma of Aeons arranged in syzygies, complementary pairs, and they would agree to an extent, and then they lay the persona of Sophia, and the emergent syzygies, over the forms. Sophia, the last and outermost Aeon, is seized by a passion to know the ineffable Father directly, without her consort, and the passion is already something, so it produces a deficiency, an abortion, and she falls from the Pleroma. In the more moderate Valentinian telling, the Demiurge forms from this, often the God of the Old Testament, ignorant but not purely malevolent, a craftsman shaping the material world from the residue of the fall, under higher guidance. Yaldabaoth is the harsher Sethian figure, lion-faced. Twist it and it would have me saying God sinned, and the sin in man mirrors out of God, and part of the promise is to redo everything without that sin. That's blasphemy.

Lurianic Kabbalah has the sefirot, and the shattering of the vessels, Shevirat HaKelim. The lower vessels could not contain the divine light poured into them, and broke. Sparks, nitzotzot, scattered and trapped among the shards, the klippot. Tikkun is raising those sparks. The same grading shows up among gnostic Christians as hylics, psychics, and pneumatics, the material, the soul-oriented, and the spiritual. The hylic end of that spectrum, in modern times, gets called NPCs.

Different languages, different people, and they are seeing the same things inside the language they know how to speak. You can't expect the divine to speak in symbols you don't have. It comes out the way the culture and the language grew, the differences they made and the differences already in the tongue. What I want to call mistakes are mostly differences. Imposing my own opinion would be that same error, so the opinion stays to the side.

In the Book of the Law the concepts are personified, gods and a cultus. A grab bag of attributes, the substance of accessibility for a human being, within our ability, within our Umwelt, the perceptual world an organism can have. We detect attributes and refine them, and ontologically group them by category of expression. Which sense, how they appear, whether they span more than one sense. Warmth is touch. Then someone says that just warms my heart. A linguistic contamination, and it expresses the attribute of warm, because love feels like that from a different sense. Out of that mess, figuring out who we are with respect to the world, cultures make gods. Following something like what Jung was thinking, I suspect there are patterns common to the human condition, a shared biological makeup. As we come up, if we are infected with language, the language starts as a tool for living together and then creeps into expressing things felt that aren't in the physical world, or things that express themselves over a sequence of time. You can look at what a nation is in the history of a nation, and group it, and you can't physically see a nation.

I thought about something versus nothing for like four months, and my description of how it could have gone comes out the same as those stories. I see it in patterns of psychology and of social groups too. That could be because it's true. It could be a shared lingual base etched into our predecessors, at the ape level, when the language was still mixed together. We have to justify the unification of things, and it bubbles up from the depth of the mind to the surface. Only so many ways that can go. What comes out is probably a bell curve, misunderstandings and better understandings of the all-thing packed across the breadth of what a human can express.

It appears, from where I'm standing, that the forms came before time and before matter. And all religions are idol worshipers. Maybe that's a bit ridiculous, but what I mean is a face gets put on. A grab bag of attributes taken for the thing, and the subset is what gets worshiped.

We even see this in the struggle over how the Trinity was made, and in the defense against the Valentinians. An ineffable god. How does it become Jesus. People say you are worshiping a man, not God, or they ask how God dies. How it came together was to say Jesus Christ was literally fully human, created in our environment, made of matter as we are, and that by the condition of his being he was able to be, or to become, fully divine, wearing the mask of the Christ. Some people are going to be upset about that. I don't care. That's how I see it. The Christ, in my mind, would have shown up even if man had not fallen. It would be a divine plan, the word from the all-thing, the one that set the structure, creating a body in which the persona of God could be made manifest. If we hadn't fallen, some other progression, the Christ shows up, and we just boogied with God all the time. Instead some fuckery changed the nature of our being. When the Christ arrived, under the law imposed by the law giver, for reasons humans can categorically never understand, he was the perfect sacrifice to undo what the action that onset the condition had done.

I have issues there. Cause if it was truly undone, was it just the law that was undone. Did God have to die to undo his own restrictions, after realizing what he had created. To say God couldn't realize it is also blasphemy. The full richness of the experience, in himself, through us, I don't know. But anyway, if it was undone and death was defeated, why do we die. Perhaps even the perfect sacrifice couldn't undo death. We were created to play out our lives in sin, and death was induced as an onset of that environment.

This starts to feel like samsara. Death and rebirth at stages of life in this environment. I think that's a recognition that the environment exists, and that the individual essence of the being might continue. A Valentinian would say yes, and that the Christ came here to spread the knowledge required to exit the environment. We are simply in. Simply in means we exist as material. When we die, the spark gets cycled back up, or imprisoned, or interrogated, offensive claims either way. Or there is a hell to pay. From a strict Lutheran standpoint that is eternal, no rebirth, forever dead, which I think is extreme. It doesn't play well with a loving God. They defer to mystery. Mystery may be the formula for exiting, keeping the kingdom on the horizon, a defense against the cognitohazard that keeps you trapped. Even discussing it would mean I'm trapped, which would be very interesting, but I don't think it's the case.

I've categorized how I see all of existence, and it's false, because there are categories I can't comprehend. You can try, and you should. The act of trying is what helps you define yourself. So, back to the all-thing and the nothing. Defining a self against the incomprehensible mirrors that refining of attributes against the absurd of nothing.

I think the question, why there is something rather than nothing, is part of a person's makeup, a moral and ethical foundation, and that it goes deeper than that. The great work is to find a passion, throw yourself into it, define for yourself where good comes from and what good is, and attempt to do good all you can. Some Christians will say that where you define good is what matters. If you take good as coming from you, your good is relative only, and will ultimately be an act of evil, really, or bad. They say man can't do good works, only God can, because anything we do for the sake of good is categorically less than God's good, God being infinite. Struggling with it, the observer trying to be good while knowing that nothing you do is ever good, will squash some people, nothing will ever work out so why try, and it will drive others to be perfectionists, perfecting the self and driving forward. A little destructive, and I don't think it's entirely wrong. You can't make everyone happy. I mean, that doesn't even apply to the great. It especially applies to what you can't comprehend. How can you expect to keep something happy if you can't comprehend it.

I assumed everything would come as the direct opposite of nothing, because that just seems to be the opposite. That rule wouldn't apply in the realm of nothingness. So why couldn't a lesser something be created. I would argue that it could, but clearly it didn't, because we have everything.
