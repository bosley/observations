# 102626 spine

Agent brief for the speech in `102626.txt`. Use it to source more examples of each move, then to rewrite that transcript as an article. Stay inside these moves. His conclusions stay his. Diction can be chosen at rewrite time; do not let diction replace a claim.

## Through-line

Most lasting trouble is a communication error. Some communication can be checked, because it sits inside shared rules. The rest is a temporal record in a living language, read by minds built out of earlier misreadings. Those misreadings arrive early, by mimicry, as definitions and as feelings, and they occupy layers. A question that cuts every layer is where people get vicious, because the definition and the feeling were never examined.

## Moves

### 1. Two kinds of reading

**Move.** Reading covers taking patterns out of a process nobody encoded, and taking meaning out of something a person wrote for another person. The first can become a testable account of a system. The second stays inside the writer and the language.

**Already in the talk.**

- Geometry as a reading of the world that becomes math.
- Reading a known system to see whether a prediction holds.
- Reading an unknown system until a theory forms, then testing it.
- Against those: material humans write for other humans.

**An added example has to show.** A regularity pulled from a process that was not a message, paired with a human account of the same kind of thing. The first can be rerun or remeasured. The second cannot.

### 2. Shared rules make a statement checkable

**Move.** Once a group shares symbols and the rules for using them, a statement inside those bounds can be checked against the rules. Agreement on process is what makes "true" operative. A record of an event has no such check. "History" here means any temporal record, including a fact written on paper today: communication with nothing behind it that a later reader can rerun. He still holds that groups can symbolize one pattern differently. Math does not escape language. The check is the rules, inside a system.

**Already in the talk.**

- Shared symbols for concepts both parties can see.
- Isolated mathematical reasoning tested against the accumulated rules.
- A written fact as the harder kind of record.

**An added example has to show.** A short formal statement a second person can recompute, beside a short factual sentence about an event they cannot recompute. Both should look equally plain.

### 3. The record is the writer's seeing

**Move.** However empirical the writer tries to be, the record is how they saw it. Words about a social structure, a motive, or a sensation do not hand over the thing. A formal description can hand over the formal object. Personal associations may differ. The object does not. Secondhand tellers can miss the truth with no intent to lie; they pass on what they understood. Enough writing can establish that people thought something happened, and still leave the state of affairs unread. A small fact, misreported in innocence, can harden into the received truth with no remainder to check.

**Already in the talk.**

- A poem of a sunset can stir a similar feeling and is not the sunset.
- A described function can be run so the other person gets the same definition and the same output. Memory may tie it to a classroom or a person. The math is the math, because the subject is the math.
- Sailors coming home after the Second World War: photos and clippings establish the event and miss what it was to meet the family.

**An added example has to show.**

- A description everyone grants is good, which still fails to deliver the experience.
- A definition someone else can instantiate and get the same result.
- A minor false detail that became the received account because the record was all there was.

### 4. The language is a second trap inside the record

**Move.** Intent aside, the language moves. A dead technical vocabulary reduces drift and does not end it. A living language fails in two shapes at once: one word with two structures, and a plain sentence whose force is social and unwritten. Time strips the social force. People near the text read it through that force. Later experts recover the words and miss the reading.

**Already in the talk.**

- Medical Latin: dead, rule-bound, a minimization of error.
- "Unlockable": there is no lock, or it is locked and cannot be opened.
- "We need to have a talk": the weight is cultural, not written in the sentence. A reader in a thousand years has no way in.
- The Iliad, as he means it: hearers near the work understood it in a way later scholarship does not recover. Nearness does the work, not expertise. He said "published"; treat that as "when it was current," not as a print event.

**An added example has to show.**

- Another word whose build allows two opposite parses.
- Another short sentence whose threat or tenderness is not in the words.
- A frozen jargon (legal, liturgical, scientific) adopted so the words would stop moving, with one ambiguity that survived.
- A second text, if the Iliad is not going to stand alone, where the first audience and later scholars clearly diverge, and the cause is proximity.

### 5. Ordinary speech fails every day, and the failure is innocent

**Move.** The utterance does not show whether the speaker has the language, is trying to be accurate inside it, or is speaking out of a mood. Mood changes what gets said. Costly formal writing suppressed that and still showed it. The average case is conversation. No liar is required. A stored feeling also discharges late, onto the wrong object, out of proportion. Same failure, delayed.

**Already in the talk.**

- Briefing the day's plans after mistaking Wednesday for Thursday. The whole account is about the wrong day.
- Two people in a house. One irritation never named. Later, at a bar, an unrelated remark detonates it. The fight is about the wrong thing.

**An added example has to show.**

- A small slip of fact (time, name, quantity) that redirects someone else's action.
- A stored irritation that lands on a nearby but different object, where both people can describe the scene and neither is describing the cause.

### 6. A person is bootstrapped, and there is no manual

**Move.** People make people with no procedure for it. The new mind is unfinished. It learns the world from the moods, half-conscious talk, and blind spots of whoever is raising it. The first meeting with a phrase, an institution, or a concept is set by how that meeting was staged, including when nobody meant to stage it. Adulthood arrives with an inner speech, a set of forms, and an identity built from context that was lived and not understood. The holes are bad definitions, and emotional weight on subjects that do not warrant it. The constructs exposed to this are large: nation, church, law, family, any hierarchy.

**Already in the talk.** The claim above. The worked cases are moves 7–9.

**An added example has to show.** An early first meeting with a large construct (a country, a church, a people, a kind of work, a parent) already loaded with someone else's unfinished feeling, which the grown person later treats as part of the construct.

### 7. A construct fires a feeling it did not earn

**Move.** Language traps forms at different scales, and their relation to a person or a group. A construct here is a loose cluster of attributions: something nebulous that a person can be seen participating in. First contact plus repeated contact bind a feeling to it. Later, the sight of the construct produces the feeling. Disgust on sight is the tell. Speech is optional. Frustration in the presence of the thing is enough. The child infers the cause and copies it. Mimicry is how the mind learns. Households pass hates this way.

**Already in the talk.**

- A cross on a neck met with seething hate.
- His setting for that hate: a country he takes as founded on Christian values; Yale, Princeton, Brown, and the other old colleges begun as denominational schools training preachers, theology first, then law and medicine. He reads the hate as generational church hurt aimed at membership.
- Old women who garden, collect for the poor, and give it away, despised by college students for the institution they belong to.
- The asymmetry he wants kept: the same vitriol aimed at rabbinic Judaism is called antisemitism, while Christ, whom he marks as a Semite, is attacked freely. He says Islam draws less of this public contempt, and he attributes that to a historical record of violence, which he separates from any given believer.
- Flat transmission: an anti-communist parent, a house that hates Christianity, a house that hates black people. The child comes out resembling the house. A lecture is not required.

**An added example has to show.**

- A visible sign (object, clothing, word, building) that triggers disgust before the particular person has acted.
- A harmless participant punished for the construct.
- A transmission with no lecture: tone, avoidance, or a face, and a child who reproduces the aversion.
- One case each for a religion, a people, and a political form, so the mechanism is not stuck to the church.

### 8. Hating the construct still builds with its pieces

**Move.** A counter-identity aimed at a dominant tradition keeps using that tradition's forms. The practice is a reply. He allows that three old, large traditions leave little unused room. His sharper observation, from people he has dealt with: they are answering the parent tradition, not starting from something they want on its own.

**Already in the talk.**

- A blanket rejection of Abrahamic religion.
- A range from the Church of Satan, taken as atheist Satanism, to theistic Satanism, still organized around Abraham.
- Magick that uses Kabbalah or Qabalah.
- The Gnostic Mass.
- Neo-pagan rites built as the inverse of what an Abrahamic tradition calls proper.
- Polytheist practice that still mirrors the refusal.

**An added example has to show.** A movement that defines itself against a parent tradition, whose rites, vocabulary, calendar, or moral inversions are legible as edits of that parent. At least one case outside this religious cluster: a politics, or a family role lived entirely as refusal of a parent. Otherwise the move looks local to his examples.

### 9. Racism, in his sense, is move 7

**Move.** He splits two things people call racism. One is the current ban on tying physical differences, including bone structure, to a race. He rejects that ban. The other is what he calls real racism: the kind visible in the 1940s, a vile feeling stuck to the construct of a race, so that seeing the form produces hate. He says that older vitriol toward black, Asian, and Hispanic Americans has not continued in the same way, and that a comparable vitriol is now aimed at white Americans, on the same pattern as hate for the church. This is not a second theory. It is move 7 with a racial construct as the object.

**Already in the talk.** The comparison above. No individual scene.

**An added example has to show.** The feeling attached to the racial construct as such, firing on sight, indifferent to the person's conduct. The claim about present contempt for white Americans needs evidence of that structure, in the same shape as the older cases. Also one non-racial case, so this move stays an instance of 7.

### 10. Five layers, and backfill

**Move.** The outlook is a stack. Lower layers are required for higher ones. Novelty at a higher layer gets a default written up from below. He calls that backfill. Backfill can be harmless. It is also how an emotional trauma is installed: a feeling written into a layer the person never examined. The weight is not only tension in the house. Parts of the structure were inferred. The mind now running on them did not put them there.

**His order, bottom to top.**

1. Ontological. Categories of being. How whatever exists is sorted, and how beings relate. "Why is there something rather than nothing" exposes defaults that were backfilled. Most people never work this layer, so it is backfilled often.
2. Evaluative, axiological. Real, good, bad, indifferent. Axioms, made or received. Intrinsic value, and patterns of indifference.
3. Ethical. Concrete moral rulings cut from layer 2. They run even when layer 2 was never judged and was backfilled from the social layer.
4. Interpersonal, social. Attachment, reciprocity, status, conflict. Inherits layer 3 and feeds it. Makes loyalty and authority loud.
5. Operational. The everyday scene: forms, objects, events, plans, the story of the self. Where attention actually sits.

**An added example has to show.** A judgment defended at layer 4 or 5 whose support is an unexamined layer 1 or 2 commitment. Show the backfill: the higher-layer situation came first, and a lower-layer answer appeared without being chosen.

### 11. One sentence can write several layers

**Move.** A small domestic line can install a picture of the good, an evaluation, and a social ethic in one stroke, into a mind too early to pull them apart.

**Already in the talk.**

- Child: why do I have to share my toys?
- Answer he disagrees with and would still give: sharing is caring.
- What lands: caring as something you do for other people; an axiological good under it; one case where an ethic applies for a social reason; and with those, an ontological picture of what good is.

**An added example has to show.** Other short lines said to children that carry a theory of the good inside a manner. Test before keeping: "because I said so," "we don't talk about that," "be nice." Keep a line only if it writes an ontology, an ethic, and a social rule together.

### 12. A question that crosses every layer makes people vicious

**Move.** Some disputes cannot be settled on the layer where they are being argued. Pressure from the operational and social layers, about which opinion you are supposed to hold, collides with the evaluative good and puts the ontological category in question.

**Already in the talk. Abortion is the worked case.**

- Rights talk as he takes it in the West: inalienable rights, bodily autonomy, freedom of choice, limited where it removes someone else's freedom.
- "My body, my choice."
- The reply he uses: another human is in the case.
- The ontological walk: not the sperm, not the egg; once they have joined, is it more than a clump of cells; is the threshold crossing the vaginal canal, which he likens to gaining a nationality by crossing a border; or is it the onset of pain.
- His position, stated apart from religious conviction: intentionally stopping development after sperm and egg have joined is murder. Lesser in degree than killing a born child or an adult. Still murder in kind, because of what the thing is.
- Intent is required for that name. A fall down the stairs that kills the child is not thereby murder.
- Edge cases do not write the rule for a society. He sets aside the rare scene, including anything that happens less than about 30 percent of the time. He reads a style of argument as building the rare scene, redefining the words, and winning inside the scene.

**An added example has to show.** Another fight that presents as policy or manners and is actually a fight about what kind of thing is in front of them. Abortion stays the primary case. The second case should force the same traversal on a different object.

### 13. Redefining the kind, from the social layer

**Move.** Ethics derived from the operational and social layers make ontological groupings available for revision, including groupings most people treat as given. The tool is a split: a biological sense of a word, and a social sense. The social sense is then reassigned to an identity. He takes "what is a woman" as the grouping under attack, and he treats the sex/gender split as that tool. Sex is the biological fact. Gender, in this use, is the social claim. His further claim: once definitions of kinds can be swapped at will, "person" is loose in the same way. He points at people in that milieu who claim animal spirits and do not count themselves as people. The culture's permission, as he has it, comes from wanting to be nice. Niceness is not love. Care would refuse the fantasy.

**Already in the talk.** That sequence. The transcript names this population with a slur. Keep the claim as hard as he made it. Pick article diction at rewrite. Do not soften the claim by cleaning the word, and do not keep the slur as if it were a technical term.

**An added example has to show.**

- A word for a natural kind split into a biological reading and a social reading, with the social reading used as the replacement.
- A defense of that replacement offered as kindness, where the cost is that other people must speak as if the grouping had changed.
