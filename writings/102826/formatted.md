# What If Everyone's Kind of Right

What if everyone's kind of right when it comes to religion and spirituality, but Christianity is the closest to what is right? What do I mean by this?

## The monad against true nothing

There is some sort of ultimate all-thing, the opposite of true nothingness. Nothingness is often hard to imagine. It is just as unknown as the all-thing, because it is the exact opposite. In this duality, whatever that is, is the ultimate monad. Through some sort of splitting, differentiation, fracturing, reflecting, refracting — through some sort of substrate of self — there are various images of it, like going through a prism and becoming separate, discernible things: categories of being, differentiated from this. The first category of being here would be the Logos, the structure of everything itself, the Word. From that, all of reality is created.

## The spark in the gradient

Human beings, made in God's image, are similar to what the Gnostics think: a divine spark in us. Perhaps. I don't know. The thing I want to hold is this. There is true, absolute nothing on the outside of that which is everything. In the gradient between everything and nothing, at the outskirts of nothing, everything becomes piled on itself and tangled through relations of self to self. That is the material realm. And us, as individual beings: our spirits are a culmination of vibrations that constitute an individual traversing the material realm. Put into the box of spacetime, over time we reveal various layers of ourselves.

A lifetime mirrors the cycle of a day. We have a dawn, we have a day, we have a dusk, and then we go into night. Or, very similarly: we are a star, and the stages of life are just our light reflected on the material world — the planet, in this metaphor — from the perspective of our current consubstantiation, the observer on the ground witnessing the light.

## The first people who could hold it

I am also somewhat convinced there is some truth to the story of Jesus, simply because of its impact on human culture and what it did. Seen through this frame, the entire world was very primitive men, and among those men there was potentially a genetic mutation in one tribe that let them see more abstractly. Perhaps it was psychedelics mixed with that. I don't know. Either way, they were able to hold higher levels of abstraction and put them to practical use than everyone else seemed to, and it is evident in their language. I am speaking of the Hebrews. Look at the complexity of the Hebrew language, even primitive Hebrew. It is fascinating. Perhaps they were the first group of humans — out of however our physical beings came into existence — able to conceptualize and communicate this information. Perhaps there is truth to the law being given. And over time they fell once again into strict interpretation.

## The letter that evades the law

There is the idea of the Shabbat goy. A Jewish person is not allowed to do anything on Shabbat, so they have a non-Jewish person with them who can do the things they are not permitted to do, provided they never explicitly ask. They might hint. As long as they never ask, they never transgress the law. This practice is not the point. It elucidates the primary issue, the core tension, that Jesus came and ultimately revealed. They were saying: of course you are following the law. But you are not meant to just follow the law. You are supposed to internalize the law.

## The sacrifice that releases the law

The way everything had been set up, there needed to be a release from that law. That release came through what René Girard saw as the sacrifice. We needed someone in the in-crowd who was just enough in the out-crowd to be sacrificed, to unify everyone toward the central theme of waking up.

Initially the Hebrews, given the law, started to wake up to the moral. They fell once again into distraction, missing the forest for the trees, pointing at the moon and mistaking the finger for the moon — however you want to phrase it. So the Logos itself created a vessel for another emanation of light, which came and was sacrificed to open the minds of the people on the planet, not just the Jewish people.

## What the opening cost

You can see it in the spawning of scholasticism, through the obsession with theology, and in the spreading of intellect through the copying of the Word and the preservation of knowledge. It really seemed to open people's minds. Over time, opening the minds and the hearts of men, we eventually started to fall away from the need of God at all, because we started to see the material realm, and we started to really understand things. That raises a question. What is the next thing going to be?

One could argue that we have not fallen at all, but have actually started to really open the mind. In this way I technically am a Christian. If I am not — I guess I don't know. But I do know that I don't think hell is for unbelievers. I think that is a made-up thing of the church. A lot of the doctrine put forward to justify the hierarchical structures that preserved the knowledge is the big miss. It is the sin they partake in.

## The pattern breaks, and keeps a steward

Martin Luther can be seen as a breaking of the pattern, similar to the showing up of Jesus, or the burning of the bush. He said: wait a minute, what are we doing here? Actually, look at the record. Let's get back to what we should be doing. But even he wanted a hierarchical structure maintained that could preserve this knowledge. Extraordinarily admirable.

## Knowledge without a guide

Now we live in a world with a wealth of knowledge. Bibles themselves are a dime a dozen. We have knowledge everywhere, but there is no real guiding force anymore. Throwing away the structure of the church, we have traded it for a soup of postmodern relativity.

The same way the early Jews had their head in the sand, looking strictly at the law and missing what the law was trying to teach, the church stuck its head in a hierarchy enforcing the law, which then emerged inconsistent, and was broken by Martin Luther. We had a big spring of the Reformation, where everyone saw new knowledge and new ways to define and delineate understandings. All of that culminated in the birth of the universities.

Right now we are seeing the edge and the dying of this, its ultimate expression. There will likely be something to herald a new age. Perhaps we will see echoes of this, the way Martin Luther was an echo of what Christ had done, which was an echo of the burning bush. We could see echoes at different scales, in the manner of prophets: people like Aleister Crowley — fuck, even L. Ron Hubbard — all trying to paint the updated picture of the total consensus.

## Seeking, and the snare

Scientology is obviously wild, and it does not have the same underlying principles as Thelema. The correlation is that there is something else going on: that there is a soul, that there is some sort of trap, or that there is a process. Either way, I am not saying Scientology is real. I am saying there was some sort of true seeing, acted upon for a purpose, based on the truth that was seen.

Things like L. Ron Hubbard are a misguided left-hand-path attempt to snare the minds of people for the pursuit of material good. In that sense, a Satanist — but not like what Crowley did. Crowley was not in it for the money, it seems. He was truly seeking enlightenment. He was weird and gross and probably not a very good guy, from what I hear. But the pursuit was not the material.

## Ignorance lifted

My stance, a sociological stance, is that perhaps Christ resurrected. I don't know. But the sacrifice done to him obviously had a profound positive effect, in a way that can be seen as the alleviation of ignorance, which is akin to the removal of sin.
