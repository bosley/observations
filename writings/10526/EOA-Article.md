# Environment, Actor, Object

Some domains of knowledge cannot be taken apart. Each piece only makes sense in relation to the whole, because the parts define one another. Recursion is built into the relations. The models may fail when pushed to an extreme — that may be a limit of the mind rather than of the model — but they are operationally valid, and so they have to be understood on their own terms.

Objects, actors, and environments are one of these domains. With the three we can model our situation as an individual. We exist in an environment made of objects we can act on. We are the actor, set apart from objects by our ability to act.

That distinction does not hold still. We can treat other actors as objects. Objects can be arranged so that they act. Groups of objects can act together inside an environment. We ourselves are actors made of objects in an environment, and the objects that compose us are an environment of their own.

## The three terms

An **environment** is a bounded group of objects. Objects and actors require one. An environment can itself be an object of a larger environment.

An **object** is a discernible thing in an environment, a form inside it.

An **actor** is one or more objects in movement within an environment, able to interact with other objects and actors by that movement. An actor need not be a mind. An actor is also a kind of environment: it has an inner and an outer environment, each made of objects, and those objects may themselves be nested objects, actors, and environments, without a final level.

## The whole and the parts

Each definition uses the other two. An environment is a group of objects. An object is a thing in an environment. An actor is objects in movement within an environment, and is an environment itself. None of the three can be understood alone. Understanding any one requires the relation among all three.

Which of the three something is depends on the environment it is considered in. We are actors in our environment. Another actor can be acted on by us as an object. The objects that compose us are an environment. The environment we are in can be an object of a larger environment. The physical things involved can be the same in each case.

## Movement and communication

To interact is to change state: to modify assignable attributes with respect to time. That happens only through action, or more simply through movement — the rearranging of objects, at some scale and to some degree, within an environment.

Actors receive information about an environment through objects. Through the movement of objects, an actor can communicate or transfer information. Communication is then an assignment of meaning to one or more series of movements, either while they are happening or after they have been captured into an environment and made still. Movement that has been made still transmits its meaning when movement is reapplied in an intended form.

A painting communicates a great deal, but one does not read a painting as one reads words, and one cannot listen to cemented brushstrokes. You have to witness it and feel it. In the same way, if you try to hear a text on a page without speaking it, you get nothing.

## Temperature and emergence

I use temperature often when explaining emergence. Individual atoms of some kind are in motion. An atom does not have a temperature. It has kinetic energy. It is an object in motion inside its environment, and therefore an actor in that environment. It may bump into another of the same kind and increase or decrease its own movement. Either way there is a transfer: a change of attributes with respect to time.

Zoom out from a bundle of particles clamoring together and a temperature can be read. That temperature belongs to a different environment. Emergence, on this view, is the consideration of the whole as an object or actor in a larger environment. The inner environment of a cloud of gas in a room may fully overlap, in physical terms, with its environment, objects, and actors. They can still be considered isolated and individual.

## Scale

The atom has kinetic energy and no temperature. The gas has a temperature once it is considered as a whole in a larger environment. Between those two considerations the scale changes, and an attribute assignable at one scale cannot be assigned at the other.

This framing of objects, actors, and environments can then be used to test claims and metaphors — other communications by actors — for a scale error in the logic.

## Open questions

What is information, and how does it relate to movement? Can we trust information that has to traverse objects to reach us? I have also not worked out how the boundary between one scale and the next is identified.
