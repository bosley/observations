# Elements

An element, an EL, is the abstract notion of a thing, purely abstract, nothing about it yet in a place, or in motion, or available to be looked at.

An object is an implementation of an EL in an environment. It can be considered by an actor, and by that same process of consideration it can be turned, arbitrarily, into an actor or into an environment. What it is depends on the consideration.

An actor is an object in an environment in motion.

## Two realms

There are two realms, mind and material. Mind is a kind of actor in an environment, and it is abstract. The realm of the material is the environment in which that actor has presence, the mind in a body.

A consideration is a functor in the realm of mind, specific to the process of guiding the mind. A process is the description of a sequential set of states in an environment. Determination implies motion, and a process may map to either realm. An event is the description of a state of affairs at some moment. In an event there are the knowns and the unknowns.

A pattern, here, is something seen in the material realm for which there is no known process by which it should emerge.

Actors then use the realm of mind to consider processes that might account for those patterns, using mind as a host for a set of considerations that guide the probing of the material, which is what tells an actor whether the considered process is correct.

## Correctness

When we look at patterns in our environment we make them known through science. We use our considerations to see whether we can reproduce the patterns, the ones seen in the material and the ones only suspected in mind.

If the suspected is then seen, and seen within the bounds of rigorous, multi-actor consideration, any one actor may take aspects of the suspected into their considerations of the known, with respect to events of a similar kind. The suspected considerations are then regarded as real.

The validity of a mental pattern is its suitability for modeling aspects of processes in the material. The models are not the process, and the labels and the dynamics we assign to objects, actors, and environments are not the real component, but that of which we are like a pointer to the real.

We use the material realm to derive our sense of correctness about these mental models. The suspicion is that this works because the mind is likely material. For our existence it has the function of an appendage of the physical being, meant to anticipate danger in an environment and to maximize the likelihood that our being continues. That couples gathering resources, predicting the processes of an environment, and the rest of what keeps a body going.

## The only one

So why are there no minds of our likeness anywhere else?

The data of the stars says there is more than enough room. The nonexistence, or the seeming nonexistence, of other minds challenges the story of materialism at the point where it asserts the rise of mind, and the rise of the being required to host that mind first.

The story implies we happen to be the only, in all of space and in present time. Maybe the first. Maybe the most recent, after the snuffing of another. But just as there seems to be no active other, there also seems never to have been another.

Language contains ambiguities because it lacks the full context in which it is used. The story of materialism contains ambiguities of the same sort, and they come from the material itself, not merely from what is known about the material.

Within the material, patterns emerge in segments of unrelated material, with no known reason. Mathematical principles are derived from these patterns, which is why mathematicians are consistently asked whether math is discovered or invented. The story of the material cannot account for these patterns, only stories told about the story of the material can, and those stories are definitionally excluded from the story of the material itself. By the story of the material I mean what we can infer about existence by witnessing the material world, now and in the past.

The existence of such patterns implies that the material is the actualization of some substance or being that is definitionally unobservable, definitionally directly unobservable. If the human mind cannot bear it, that is no structural failing of the mind but a substance failing, since the condition of being a human which contains the mind is rooted in the downstream composition of that actualization, much like how you can't tell an entire story inside a single word, a story being a composition of words.

## Actualization

Actualization is a trap door in the language. It may carry in the notion of an actualizer. That may be a failing of the word, and the thing might be better described as a process. But observer, process, and actualizer are all still downstream. On the story of materialism they are a subset of the material, since that story states that the mind is contained in the material. From the stance of the material, one can't account for any abstractions at all.

Modern thinkers say, therefore, that abstractions don't exist. They are useful illusions, derived from reality, that predict the course of reality, and don't ultimately exist. It can seem enlightened to adopt this. It may be a failure of my mind's capacity or structure, but it feels absurd. Something we can see, derive, and use every day to predict reality is supposed to not exist.

I don't suspect that everything is an illusion. It is consistent, though, to operate inside science as the leveraging of illusions: illusions that inform action, that produce patterns, that can be said to justify further illusions. The same measure can be leveraged toward the divine. If all of it is a measure of useful illusion, then adopting an illusion to fulfill a use is consistent, so long as a God in that illusion does not undermine the other assertions the materialist is standing on.

Social stigma modulates that consistency. It contains the usefulness, to a degree that adopting the illusion is often not beneficial. The exception is someone attempting to undermine a religious institution, where the materialist stance can be used against the core tenets of that institution while staying one foot inside the cultural norm. Churches like the ELCA, and other far-left implementations of otherwise conservative theologies, are the case.

The adoption of these illusions is the mechanism by which the postmodernist procedure killed God, leveraging reason and reproducibility. The mind of man has invented a self-described system that doesn't exist in order to justify that God doesn't exist. I don't think this has been the direction of one mind. It is a multigenerational layer of principalities that humans have found themselves in throughout the struggles of their existence (see: https://beausol.ai).

## A Problem

The sharper problem is that the materialist cannot account for the full picture, cannot account for the mind, and that this might be a failure of the axiomatic implication that material comes before mind, though how could one possible assert that mind comes before material? 

## An Open Question

Is it sensible to model these tools as illusory?

Calling them illusions keeps the materialist story intact, since from inside that story abstractions cannot be accounted for at all, and it keeps science operating, since science can be described as the leveraging of such illusions to inform action that produces patterns. It also, by the same measure, licenses other illusions, including the divine, which is then kept out by the social stigma of the model rather than by the logic.

What would count as evidence for something that accounts for the fullness of the material realm, that removes the oddness of calling a thing an illusion when we can actually use it, and that gives some clarity on the connection between what we can dream to be real and what can be said to be real?
