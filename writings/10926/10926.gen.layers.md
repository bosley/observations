# Layers, depth, and the turn

## How to read this

This is taken from the source transcription of this dictation only. His stacks are local: the dog-and-bed hops, the story’s unstated context, the simultaneous layers inside self-remembrance, the depths of help, and the person as a brain driving a body are not one numbered system. Where the speech-to-text was uncertain, the mark stays a mark. It is not treated as doctrine.

## Layers and depth

### Attributes, hops, and resolution

Looking at his dog on a bed, he does not name the parts first. He feels a thing with four legs, then notices the dog also has four legs, and knows those thoughts are “not related at all.” The joke-work at a bar runs on the same mechanism. A collection of objects, bounded by whatever his attention is on, shows relations, and the relations come in layers: the object, its attributes, then other objects searched out from those attributes. Four more legs, and the question “what’s that on?” lands on the bed.

He does not only look at the thing or its attribute. He takes a snapshot, then either goes into an attribute to “gain higher resolution on that subset,” or hops to the next object on whatever top-layer attribute he just attached. Higher resolution means looking closer, physically, or bringing the thing into consideration more. The wander usually starts at the intersections of two subjects.

### Story as unstated layers

He bridges that into storytelling with the line “Jack and Jill went up a hill to get a bucket of water.” The story has no conclusion. It might be old, ongoing, or “a total fib.”

Names arrive already sexed. Jack is a male signal impressed over a lifetime; Jill is “boom, girl.” He treats that as biology, and he is blunt about people who call it a problem. Brother, sister, friends, mother and son: none of that can be inferred. The top objects are Jack, Jill, hill, bucket, water. The bucket is a holder of water. The hill is a landscape they have to traverse upward. Standing alone, the line ends, and the mind asks where it has seen two people go up for a bucket of water, and why.

Context decides the reading. If a joke is coming, he waits for something to happen between the two and the bucket: forgotten, filled with something else, a relation an adult audience would hear. If it is a fairytale for coming-of-age teens, the adult commentary drops out, and Jill might be a savior while Jack poisons the well. “We don’t really know.” The context around the objects is what gets imprinted, including adjacent context, not only the sentence in front of him.

A burned dinner carries the same shape. A mother mad about messed-up food may have left a weight where burning or wasting food now feels like harming the people around you. Food aggression, people furious if you ask to share, he likens to dogs and a childhood trigger. He also allows that he is mistaken, and that the person is fine. While he listens he holds the top objects and a side context: what this person has said before that was not preloaded here. A sister who says nothing about homework on the art table, then blows up at school, is his analogy for an ongoing weight under a somewhat adjacent context. Upset at someone lazy and stupid may be fear of being that, or blame carried over from someone else who was. “You bring your baggage to everything you say.” Past and present sit around forms beneath the current speech. Those forms show more if you ask whether a private conversation, or an older one, would have given more. “It’s hard to see our own.” He stops there: “where am I going with this I think.”

### Simultaneous layers inside self-remembrance

He ties the next stack to self remembrance: in the moment you should observe yourself observing, and “see your true self,” there are multiple layers to look at.

The physical layer is atoms, “just physicalism,” at a resolution he does not fully have. He understands gravity and weight by intuition, and knows they are at work. Above that, what the organization implies, and how the things relate — he calls this an “illogical overlay,” left as heard, sense unclear. Context then narrows which resolution is useful. The computer’s physical nature is not helpful while debugging; increasing resolution there means narrowing the problem set. Cutting chicken, you can know proteins, enzymes, and the farm it came from, and still not dwell on mass farming. That is “just a downer.” People who stay there, “and Peter or whatever,” end up vegans, “and that’s fine,” but you do not need that concern. Know it is the case and move on. He wants “a comfortable depth” with regard to what you are looking at. The useful resolution may be: physical matter in front of me, cooking this chicken for my family. Context brings up the next layer.

### Why you are there, and for whom

He then calls the case for why you are there an ontological layer. For most people that case is relations to other people. Garage work is a second-order relation: he engages other people for money, and the money is for his family, so “I’m here for my family.” Groceries, picking something up, cleaning, resource collection for the group or for yourself — “it’s always around the relations to other minds.” The questions stack: what is in front of me, how does it work together, why am I here, for whom am I here.

### Restatement while still acting

He restates while insisting you stay in the act. Hold the layers “to a specific resolution, all simultaneously, without detaching.” When he sits with a problem and does nothing else, the breakdown is: the problem; the physical context he is working in; the forms and how they relate, at a limited depth so he does not get sidetracked; then systems, grouped so he can know their behavior; then why we are here, what constructs are going on, and how they are working. Once he has the material reason he is there, he will “end up throwing away that layer,” but it stays “part of the onboard process.” Thrown away is not gone.

This restatement is his own division of the moment. It is not the selfishness stack, and it is not the brain-and-suit image.

### Depths of selfishness and help

Separately, he walks help as depths of selfishness. A very selfish person, asked to change a tire at an inconvenient hour, says no and does not go. That is layer one. Next, help for pay, or the thought that if I do this they will do that for me — sole motivation, still selfish. Next, help because they helped before and “I feel like I have to”: no future return expected, but a past perceived debt. That is also selfishness. Truly selfless help is helping because the person needs help. You stop making thoughts about it. You may recognize the other factors, but if they never cross the mind, “that’s pure selflessness.”

Reflection can spoil the last category. You think you are there, then see you are in the second or third. “It’s really all about the levels of which we care to look.” As a finite being — he does not think he needs a proof that he is not infinite — there is only so much attention he can give in a moment, and only so many levels he can operate at once.

His suspicion about some autism sits beside that limit, not inside the help-stack. He does not know. He suspects some physical defect, and models a mind whose relations, when the environment changes — people who cannot walk through doors — shift so hard that the mental landscape induces panic. “A bajillion,” then “1 million,” reasons. He is modeling a thought space, not diagnosing.

### Person-scale and life-scale resolution

The image that started the closing stretch, while he was with the dog on a lunch break, is different again. Not engaged in an act, he sees a person as a brain and nervous system “driving the suit of the body”: eyes dangling off a brain, the nerves grotesque, ready to come up. Mind seems bioelectric. He is not saying we only reside in electricity; “a bundle of wet electricity,” propelling around the fields, and the old talk of a spark, of something animated or charged. He has seen someone die. The light seems to leave through the eyes. Functions you cannot name cease, and the substrate changes in a way you perceive. Relation to the person can give that an extreme emotional pull. Witnessing it is “like seeing electricity leave.”

Death, for him here, runs back to sperm and egg, cells moving and spinning — he said “spitting,” and splitting or spinning is unclear — a spark from the parents, and genes as “a map that grow a thing.” He will not claim other attributes come with that spark. The same people who give the electricity then raise you, and through language and forms impress ways to express the genetics. A baby from 1000 BC, given modern nutrition, could be taught modern physics. The capacity looks “absurdly disproportionate” to survival: planning for winter, farming, better tools, and then microprocessors and photolithography. Perhaps purpose is not simply to survive. He calls the survival-only reading reductionist.

From “first principles of mind,” observation without certain levels of resolution is enough for a life. Basic mathematics covers most positions; advanced trigonometry still exists and still impacts you. Refining attributes can miss “the bigger picture of what existence is.” A scientist who calls the bed a blanket is a weak joke. The better one is the rose handed over on a birthday, answered with “why’d you give me plant material?” Hyper-analyzing some attributes and ignoring others is allowed. What determines care is “picking and choosing which relations and how deeply you want to bring them to resolution.”

He then hands the last paragraphs to the bot and asks for coherence. This file does not finish that ask as a new claim.

## Left / right, choice, and self-remembrance

### Self-remembering and self-remembrance

He was reading someone who made an approach called self remembering. Speech-to-text lost the name — “Jeff GURJ,” “kerchief,” “Kerr” — and he expected a bot to recover it. It stays unintelligible here.

The method, as he has it: you find yourself in an action, take a moment to feel it, then direct observation not only at what you are doing but “back in on the self,” so you observe yourself observing while you are doing it. To him it feels like being in the moment. “Maybe it’s more profound than that. I don’t know.” His own variant, which he thinks comes from the nature of his work, does not stop at looking at himself looking. He sorts objects, attributes, and relations, and the multiple layers belong in that same moment. He uses both “self remembering” and “self remembrance,” and they are not collapsed here.

The site he names, Beausolai.ai as heard, and the tractate of agreeable statements, are how he frames the method of the philosophy, not a layer in the stacks above. Points of disagreement are where the views come apart.

### The split at “Who am I here for?”

“Who am I here for?” is where he says world views split.

The left-hand path, in his telling, is people who dress in robes, cut their facial hair into points, and rename themselves Lilith. What they are expressing is counterculture: other minds are here, but the focus is “some aspects of me as an individual,” “the art of crafting the individual.” He reaches for apophatic philosophy as the way to define a concept you cannot quite grapple with, and treats counterculture as a physical emergence of that. Culture is what was imprinted; the person rejects it and dons symbols, makeup, masks, and activities to strengthen an identity against those norms. Get enough of them together and he sees an Anton Levi [likely LaVey] situation, “Levi and Satanism,” using ritual and mockery to establish an atheist identity. Fascinating, he says, and “just a waste of time.” If you want to be an individual, be one. He does not see a need to project it on others. That is his costume-reading. It is not a scholarly account of those traditions.

The right-hand path, at that same split, is “removing this self”: I have a self, but every time I work for myself, I fail. He hears Christianity say God takes broken men, and finds us at our lowest, and he reads that as people who had been operating through a left-hand illusion, or something generally left-handed — not necessarily Satanism, just “choosing to be yourself.”

### Conditions of the choice

The conditions he names are comfort, no friction, and no identity. In an individualist culture, as “the church starts getting out of the home,” people grow up without a cultural purpose ahead of them and go to whatever is in the room: pornography, drugs, sometimes self-study. Thinking this hard needs a reason. He does not know if that reason is environmental or evolutionary. He does know there has to be a friction point. A comfy environment that says “go be an individual,” with nothing to tell you right or wrong except “what you can get away with,” produces what you can get away with. A mirror can cue the turn — physical, or metaphysical. On self-analyzation, someone says they have been doing left-handed things, have been selfish, and it is time to give up because they cannot do anything right, and then they “arbitrarily follow that set of rules.” That is how he will articulate what he has observed. He is not sure it is more than that.

Why you are there can sit between happening to be there and having crafted the situation so it is exactly where you wanted. He leaves the in-between open.

### His stance

He does not want emotional baggage on which hand is correct. “It’s just simply which way the observation is pointing.” He does not care what people do, and he will mock them “for being characters of themselves.”

In programming, left versus right is not selfishness. It is front end or back end, the task, and how deep you open an object. A bloated object ships fifty things when three are needed; you do not give the others cognitive worth — he said “worse,” and worth or weight is unclear — until you need them. He had “dehydrate” a bunch of UI components; hydrate is the likely word, and it stays marked. Apply that to why am I here, who am I, what am I doing. “Who am I doing this form?” is resolved, once, toward the nearby “for whom,” and marked as interpretive.

The personal question that returns is not a programming question: refine the individual self, or open up, let go, and work selflessly for those around you. It need not be Christian. Growing up he was told “that’s just what you do.”

## What this file does not settle

There is no single layer taxonomy in the dictation. Photolithography is an example of capacity beyond what survival seems to require, not a name for a layer. The ending was left for a bot to make coherent. It is not completed here as a new claim.
