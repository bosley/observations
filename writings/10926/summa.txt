Forms are to minds what math is to the physical world. Math is abstracted from the regularities of the physical world, set down in notation, and the world then answers to it. Forms work one level in: relations like force, limit, debt, witness, and return are abstracted from what minds do and leave behind in speech, ritual, and artifact, and minds then answer to them. A mind is made of forms, and it sits in space.

Cultures and languages cut these forms differently, and the cuts overlap. Quechua grammar requires a speaker to mark whether they saw something, heard it, or inferred it, so witness is built into the sentence, while English leaves it optional. Nearly every language speaks of time in spatial terms, but Aymara puts the past ahead and the future behind, where English does the reverse. Korean makes the tight or loose fit of one thing in another the basic spatial contrast, where English asks only whether something is in or on. The same relations surface everywhere, each language bringing a different part of them forward. The overlap comes from two sources. Bodies meet frictions that are alike in kind, such as gravity, hunger, weather, scarcity, and death, so the same relations get worked out again. And the biological structures that meet them are alike in kind, so the same shapes are easy to hold. What crosses between minds is the structure. The experience itself stays with the individual alone.
