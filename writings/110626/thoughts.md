Recently I shut my eyes. I was alone and comfortable and I didn't need anything, and the question there was who am I, which people don't tend to ask. Nothing was coming in, so what I had were thoughts, bills, something in the family, whatever had weight on it, and not hunger, since hunger would have put me out of the chair. A small child alone has that tension moving in him and does not ask why it is there. If I sit down meaning to see what comes up, I can look at the thought and ask why, and when I don't know why it is happening then it is happening to me, and it is also coming from me. It gets heady, so I won't stay in it.

I can hold things in mind that are not real the way a rock is real. A number is how a quantity gets tracked. I can put a mark for three on something, or make up a new mark, and the three is not in the pile of rocks, though the pile can be counted. I suspect the mind is on the brain in that relation. Molecules lined up in a way nobody can fully lay out, a flood of signal, and out of that a something that can notice itself, and I don't know where in the lineup the noticing happens. I've been calling that something a complex nothing. I don't mean absolute nothing, the absence of everything and of logic too, which can't be described. I've heard it put that true nothingness is the thing rocks dream of, and I think that's the bottom. Above it there are thinner layers, quantities, thoughts, thoughts about thoughts, and the arrangements a person is already living in without looking at them, none of them in the material the way a rock is, and a life gets steered by them.

An ant colony has a direction the single ant does not have. The ant has a role, and the colony's work gets done through ants who don't hold the colony's picture. People look like that to me, agents of a similar enough kind, under some loose set of rules, wherever they happen to be interacting, settling into clusters. What I think I see is the cluster acting through the people carrying it, the way the mind acts through the brain. The word I've been using is principality.

We live in a pile of these already. Family, city, state, nation: very different people, grouped so roads get built and food moves and strangers can occupy one place. A club or a church is one a person chooses, a way of living with other people and a way of seeing, and the seeing comes with a way of behaving. Put a thousand people who hold the same tenets among people who don't, and a pattern shows up and a routine follows, flavored by the person, some of it on purpose and a lot of it not, going on whether or not anyone asked why they were in it.

I think the old gods were names for this at the size of one person. Someone in 350 BC who can't hold his attention, walking, pulled by something bright, then a butterfly, then something else, and if he catches himself it is happening to him and he doesn't want it. Mercury is not a foolish name for that from where he was standing. Anger that shows up like a visitor and wants him to go beat someone gets called Mars, or some bad spirit. What we'd say now is that something unresolved is coming up into the working mind. From where he stood, something was doing it to him. I don't think that was a wrong way to say it, and the later change was in the name.

God is dead, we have killed him. I think the line is true and not true together, which is hard to say when a sentence is built to be one or the other. For a long time the gods were how a person in that watching state understood what was happening to him. In the West the crowd of them gave way to one God, and a lot of the explaining was taken into the one, and universities were made, and scripture, medicine, and law turned toward the study of creation, alchemy toward chemistry and physics. The finer the looking, the harder it was to start from the theologies people held and reach them from first principles, and what could be measured came to be treated as the whole of what there was. The things that had been called demons, and whatever had led people to build theologies, were still plaguing them, and secular speech had no way to say it. Psychology is what that inward turn produced, and Jung's archetypes are a taxonomy of the subdivisions of human agency.

Luther looked at his own church, at the people in it, and they were doing things that did not match what they said they thought. St. Peter's was being rebuilt with indulgences, tickets out of purgatory, and branches of that body were yielding terrible fruit, priests in brothels among them. He called them hypocrites and whitewashed tombs, the charge Jesus put on the Pharisees, and he aimed it at his own house. I don't need his conclusions to have been the right ones. He went back over what was written and asked why they were doing these things when they said they thought those things, which is the same question as why these thoughts are here when it isn't really me, and then he stated again what he believed the thing was and tried to live toward that.

Once it had been said in public, other places agreed that something was wrong and said he was wrong about what to do instead. Sweden, Germany, toward Rome, people with their own languages and their own way of already being together, and when they reflected they came out differently, each painting the picture as it saw it correct, and those look to me like different shades of a principality. Theology was a problem to be solved, a disconnect between what was done and what was said, and the means they used on it were the means they used on nature. I don't want that pinned to a denomination. The difference looks like a modality of mind, and after the break the science out of the north doesn't look like the science out of the south. The early universities come later, each a branch of a way of thinking already in use.

When that mystery comes off, the pressure it supplied has to be carried by something else. Family and meaning were carrying part of it. A secular common life, as far as I can see, offers measurements where there used to be tenets, and people move in the older arrangement. I suspect the classical idea of America died before I was born, and that a lot of what I've lived in is what it left, the West looking the same way to me, the account having come apart from the conduct and people having gone on with the conduct, not that there's no hope in saying so.

We say we are endowed with certain inalienable rights, by our Creator. I think that's a strong ground for a morality, and it has the rights coming from a God a lot of people in the arrangement no longer believe in, so why people have rights at all can be asked. I think that question is dangerous with nothing under it. If you can justify people not having them, you can justify a great deal I call horrible, and horrible, as I mean it, is tuned to an older Western sense of good and bad. There are groups that never set the tenets down. They say what they are doing and why, Catholics and Jews and Muslims who practice among them. A lot of the rest of us stepped out. Church or not, school or not, a job because people have jobs, roles pushed on us and roles we take up, thoughts and thoughts about thoughts that nobody examined, so that without the examination a person is in a stream.

We are agents, and we engage in things without full understanding, and when we error it is almost always detrimental. I think that is what Christians have been saying the whole time. The way I've heard Jordan Peterson put it is that the Bible isn't necessarily even true, it's the set of preconditions on which you can gauge truth. I don't know what would backfill the pressure and realign people, and a large conflict will not do it.
