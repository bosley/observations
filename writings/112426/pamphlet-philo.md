# Pamphlet Philosophy: Nine Plain Truths

*Read it in the time it takes to board.*

---

**1. We are a different kind of animal.**
Language, tools, reach. No other creature on this earth does what we do.

**2. Born. Aging. Dying. No exceptions.**
Medicine buys time. It doesn't buy out.

**3. Deep time lies on both sides of us.**
Before us, only stories. After us, it will be as if we never were. We weren't there at the start, and we won't be there at the end.

**4. To live is to choose.**
Nobody opts out. Not choosing is a choice.

**5. Act as if you're free.**
Maybe we are. Maybe we're just along for the ride. Either way, the choice lands on you, so own it.

**6. Loving for them and loving for you can be the same love.**
Wanting the best for someone is pure. Enjoying their best is no less pure. Forcing your "best" on them is not love, even if it starts there.

**7. Everyone lives inside their own picture of the world.**
Two people, one street, two different worlds. Swap their lives and each would be shaken to the bone. Don't mistake your picture for the world.

**8. Standing in another's shoes is the human trick.**
We can hold a view that isn't ours. That's what sets us apart, and most of us only do it when pushed. So push yourself.

**9. You are what you enjoy.**
The circus isn't only for fools. It's wonder, awe, and play, and no one's off the hook. What a person delights in tells you who they are.
If you felt a sense of judgement from this statement, ask yourself what is it you're enjoying that you don't "want to idenitify with." 

---

### Pocket Card (slogans only)

1. A different kind of animal.
2. Born. Aging. Dying.
3. Deep time on both sides.
4. To live is to choose.
5. Act as if you're free.
6. Love doesn't force.
7. Everyone has their own picture.
8. Stand in another's shoes.
9. You are what you enjoy.

---

**Notes on choices I made:**
- I swapped the vocabulary that carries modern baggage ("meta-cognition," "deterministic," "world model") for plain words like *picture of the world* and *stand in another's shoes*.
- Mrs Maam and Mr Sir got compressed to a single line. They work in a longer essay, but a pamphlet needs the conclusion, not the thought experiment.
- Point 1 will probably do the most to provoke people. Point 9 is the riskiest as a slogan, because "you are what you enjoy" can read as a judgment, so you may want to soften or sharpen it depending on the reaction you want.
