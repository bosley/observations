I want to express my beliefs not using the language of modernity. Staying away from, or intelligently leveraging the baggage and issues that cling onto the shared experience of language in the west. It comes down to making a series of choices at various scales. Presentint the phenomena that is the forcing functions making the choices relecant. I want to place it, positin myself, in such a way that it can be found agreeable to the priests of the body, as well as priests of the culture. In order to get through to anyone right now, a base-line almost dead-simple or stupid way to express simple truths. It needs to be antagonistic in a way that causes people to be in controlled uproar, and thats not something I can manufacture. Instead, I will write things plainly and let them sink into time.

1) Humans are distinct, in some way, from other species on this planet

Language, technology, global dominance among other species. We are clearly well suited to the planet's current conditions and are in a league of our own compared to other species.

2) We are born, we age, we die, and there is no ability to change these conditions of mortality. 

The medical industry and knowledgebase is substantial, but can not be reasonably hedged upon that they will deliver ideal immortality before you are to expire. There is no escape.

3) Unfathomable depths of time exist at our boundaries. 

Before us only, a set of stories and goods-produced, after us, so much time and change will pass that "it will be as if we never existed" as all record of the individuak will, meterially, be lost to time.

We have no idea about what "truly" happened before we are born, we weren't there.

4) we find ourselves having to make choices every day

no matter what scale of life youre operating in primarily you, as a consequence of being alive, are forced to make choices. everyone is subjected to this and it feels overtly obvious to state such.

5) we may or may not have free will, but operationally we should assume we do.

Whether or not our next move is deterministic and we are "just along for the ride" is ultimately a nothing burger. No matter if our will is free or not we are subjected to choices and we must instill responsibility on ourselves for that in which we partake. to do otherwise would call into question our ability to rule ourselves at all.

6) Own interest and other interest sit on a gradient and can be described in ways that look opposed without either description being false.

You can make the argument that everything every person does is "selfish" meaning to say "in their own interest." The pesimist will take this to absurdities and they often leverage this to spread existential heartache like nobody loves you for you, they love you for them, etc. There can be truth in that. One can love someone for how they make them feel. That is a big part of love, but that doesn't make the love any less authentic, why should it? Wanting the best for other fully for the sake of the other is a pure love. Wanting the best for the other, and deriving enjoyment from them achieving their best is no less pure. Forcing what you think is best on another against their will is a tainted love. It may originate in love, but it does not express love.

people don't always feel drawn towards the "whys" that underly their behaviours, sourced from their world view. every human operates in some internally-modelled world in which they see themselves in. the form this takes is likely unique to each individual, clustering similarities in overall types no-doubt. however it works, all humans model the world to some degree.

these models can be so different that it might seem alien if you could suddenly swap-out deep-rooted perspectives on someone by "administering experience" in such a way that the deeply different perspectives of the world could be truely and deeply understood. 

imagine for instance we take hypothetical individuals; first we have a middle-school flowery teacher, Mrs Maam. 
Mrs Maam is a late 20-something freshly out of college, music teacher. She enjoys a light-hearted and sincere lifestyle. Middle-class American lifestyle growing up, football games, and trick-or-treating. 

next up we have Mr Sir. Mr Sir, also a 20-something didn't have such luck growing up. Mr Sir grew up in South East Asia and witnessed his parents get murdered by political extremists when he was 12. Now, Mr Sir carries a rifle the way Mrs Maam carries a smile, as part of their identity. 

If we were to somehow subject Mrs Maam to the full life experience of Mr Sir, not just a dictation of facts, but to subject her whole person to the full breadth and depth of experience of Mr Sir in some scifi fasion, then it would be fair to say that she might be traumatized. what "just another day" for Mr Sir is, can be gut-renching horror to Mrs Maam. If you were to do the same to Mr Sir, what would his response be? He has already stood the test of trauma, what then if you subjected him to the love of a stable middle american family? Might he suddenly don a since of guilt and conscious that was so alien to him it never could have existed if not for this experiment? Perhaps he too would be similarly traumatized then. 

What if Mr Sir has an operating IQ of 70, while Mrs Maam was about 120. In this experieriment, would Mr Sir's biology be able to replicate the understanding of experience sufficiently or would the device also need to perform cognitive corrections to bestow upon him a higher sense of reasoning for the duration of the experience, only to be brought back down after. How would he handle that?
