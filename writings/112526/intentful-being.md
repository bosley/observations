Every reader of this sentence exists in some way. None of us can remember the time before birth or anticipate what comes after death, so existence presents itself as a succession of moments, each experienced as a present. The continuity of that succession is what we identify as the self.
Each present arrives within a context: a task at work, a duty at home, or an activity chosen when circumstances are relaxed. The context acts as a filter on what is experienced, and together with our intentions it determines the decisions we face. Whether those decisions are made freely is a separate question. What remains constant in experience is that a choice must be made.
Agency can be understood as the capacity to comprehend the context in which one's present arrives, and from that to identify the actions available within one's perceived authority. In organizations the term is used more narrowly. A person is said to act with agency when they take it upon themselves to resolve a problem they saw coming, and when the action benefits the formal hierarchy they belong to. By this usage, agency is measured by its usefulness to those who set the terms of the context.
