# The Evolution of Shared Forms: Communication and the Compression of Gods

## The voice in the Iliad

In the oldest strata of the Iliad there is almost nothing that we would recognize as an interior. Nobody sits down and deliberates. When a hard choice arrives, it arrives as a voice, and the voice is a god, and it is obeyed. Julian Jaynes read the poem as a psychological document, arguing that the words which later carry mental meaning — *psyche*, *thumos*, *phren*, *noos* — are still concrete in Homer: breath and blood, agitation in the midriff, seeing [(1)](#r1)[(2)](#r2). His proposal was that volition in that period was organized without consciousness and then delivered to the person in the person's own language, sometimes with the aura of a familiar authority, sometimes as a voice alone [(3)](#r3).

The likeness Jaynes reached for was clinical. He compared the experience to the auditory hallucinations of schizophrenia, and he leaned on the detail that such voices are taken as authoritative rather than weighed, that to hear is already to obey [(3)](#r3). I take his direction and decline the word, which carries an implication of malfunction. The gods, on this reading, are what a process looked like from the inside before there was anything we would call a self: someone observing an environment, in a brain that did not yet have language adequate to what it was seeing.

In 1886 Nietzsche proposed that consciousness developed only under the pressure of the need for communication, that it was useful in the first instance between people rather than within one, and that the development of language and the development of reason becoming aware of itself proceed together [(4)](#r4). He also proposed the reason it would be so: the human animal was the endangered one, needed help, and therefore had to be able to say what was wrong.

## Relations as the unit, and the gap between minds

The account requires a unit smaller than an idea. When a mind sets a subject beside an object, or holds two separate things together, it makes a link between them, and that link can then be linked to further links without any obvious ceiling. I do not think most of us work more than one or two layers out from the ground; past that the returns fall off sharply for the effort spent. There are failures in the senses, and no position from which the whole arrangement can be inspected from outside, and nothing in the structure delivers truth. What can be graded is divergence: how far one account has come apart from another, and how far both have come apart from what is left in the world to be examined, and the links that last are the ones several minds can carry in nearly the same shape.

No mind reaches another directly. The body has to be used — voice, gesture, mark — to get the link across the gap, and the receiving mind has to reconstruct it from the signal. Where speech is not yet adequate to the link, the link gets put onto something that outlasts the speaker [(4)](#r4).

## The external record

The Gravettian female figurines are the earliest sustained case of a shape held in common and made portable. The Venus of Willendorf, roughly 30,000 years old, is eleven centimetres of oolitic limestone whose raw material is not local to the site, carved with the reproductive body pronounced and the face absent, the head worked instead into concentric bands read variously as plaited hair, a headdress, or a woven cap [(5)](#r5)[(6)](#r6)[(7)](#r7). The museum holding it notes that faceless execution is a specific statement, since modelled faces occur elsewhere in the same broad tradition, and that the wide geographic spread of the type indicates a symbolism that was understood across it [(6)](#r6).

Göbekli Tepe in southeastern Turkey is the same act at a scale that required organization. The early layer dates to the Pre-Pottery Neolithic, the second half of the tenth and the ninth millennia BC, before domesticated plants and animals, and it produced T-shaped monolithic pillars in circular enclosures around taller central pairs, carved with foxes, snakes, scorpions, boars, aurochs, birds, and in places with arms and hands that mark the pillars as stylized human-like figures [(8)](#r8)[(9)](#r9). Klaus Schmidt read the site as a hill sanctuary rather than a settlement, a regional place where communities came together, with evidence of feasting and probably of beer from fermented wild cereals, and with the enclosures deliberately and rapidly backfilled after a period of use [(8)](#r8)[(9)](#r9).

I read both as teaching objects. A group that has found a shape several of its minds can hold will lose it when the people who found it die, unless it has been put into stone, into a story told the same way twice, or into a sequence of acts performed in the same order. Merlin Donald's account of cognitive evolution describes this layering: a mimetic stage of represented skill, then a mythic stage in which spoken language organizes collective knowledge through narrative and ritual, then a stage in which external symbolic records — what he calls exograms — take over storage from biological memory and change the architecture within which thinking happens [(10)](#r10).

## Complexity, friction, and the specialists

A group grows in headcount, in the tangle of relations among its members, and in how those relations meet the material conditions around them — where the food is, what the season does, which neighbours are nearby and what they want. A way of talking can be adequate to a given level of that tangle and sit there for a long while. Then a season fails, or a drought runs long, or another group arrives with practices that do not parse. The friction forces either a more precise shared way of speaking or the dissolution of the group.

Ritual is one of the products of that pressure. A death removes a member and leaves consequences distributed across everyone who depended on the arrangement; unmanaged, that is a fracture. A fixed sequence that walks the survivors through it is mitigation installed in advance. Durkheim's formulation is the one I want here: religious representations are collective representations that express collective realities, and rites are ways of acting that arise in the midst of assembled groups and exist to evoke, maintain, or recreate the mental states of those groups [(11)](#r11). He added the stronger claim that a society cannot leave the shared categories to individual choice without abandoning itself, and that a minimum of logical conformity is a condition of social life at all [(11)](#r11).

The specialists follow from the same pressure. Priestly rank goes to the people who can hold several accounts of one event at once, gauge across them, and tell the group what the event requires. Where the experience of the period was something like an authoritative voice, what they had to say, in whatever words that stage of language allowed, was that they saw what the other was seeing and knew what it demanded.

Geographically separated groups arrived at gods without contact between them. The remark I first met, that there are no atheist tribes, is stronger than the record. Tylor built his minimum definition of religion as the belief in spiritual beings and reported that it appeared among every society he knew with any intimacy, while explicitly refusing to call it instinctive or to claim that a pre-religious condition was impossible, on the grounds that the negative reports were unreliable rather than conclusive [(12)](#r12). Durkheim treated the collective representation as socially indispensable [(11)](#r11). The most cited modern candidate for an exception, the Pirahã, is contested: Daniel Everett reports no indigenous creation narrative and no supernatural belief, and other researchers in the cognitive science of religion read the same ethnography, with its spirits that take the shape of things in the environment, as religious [(13)](#r13)[(14)](#r14).

## Why separated groups arrive at similar shapes

If the brain came to be through something like the process we call evolution, and if it is the host of thought, then some of what appears in a mind may be there because of how the substrate is built. I think of how single crystals are grown for chips: polysilicon melted in a quartz crucible, a small seed of known orientation dipped to the surface and then withdrawn slowly while both rotate, the material solidifying onto the seed and inheriting its orientation, the resulting boule sliced into the wafers everything else is built on [(15)](#r15). Something analogous inside the substrate — a repeated underlying pattern, whatever sets it — would mean that certain shapes are available to be found in any human mind.

Pascal Boyer's experimental result, which is narrower than the substrate conjecture, is that religious concepts across cultures violate expectations at the level of broad domains such as person, animal, or artifact, that such violations are recalled better than ordinary associations, and that the advantage depends on the concept still supporting inference, which is why religious concepts almost never stack many violations at once [(16)](#r16). He argues that religious thought recruits mental systems that are present anyway, which is why recurrent features show up in cultures with no contact [(17)](#r17).

On the capacity itself I hold a conjecture I cannot test. What gets called intelligence now is the ability to keep high resolution on a strictly bounded set of relations. I think the underlying quantity is closer to evenly distributed than that measurement suggests, and that what differs is the shape of the vessel it sits in — set by biology and by everything the environment and the person have inscribed on each other — so that the same amount either spreads across breadth with little depth anywhere or runs down a narrow path and goes far, with injury that destroys the substrate as an edge case.

The folk notion that genius sits a step from madness registers, I think, a person working in relations complex enough, or far enough from the local norm, that the surrounding minds cannot track, or a person positioned so that a set of relations happens to be illuminated which was otherwise dark.

## Planning across scales

The tradition collected under the name of Hermes Trismegistus reads, to me, as an early statement of this structure. The formula usually quoted, "as above, so below," is a modern paraphrase of the second verse of the Emerald Tablet, a short text that first appears in Arabic in the late eighth or early ninth century; the Latin has that which is above being like that which is below, while the Arabic has the two being *from* each other [(18)](#r18). The philosophical Hermetica, the Corpus Hermeticum, is a separate Greco-Egyptian body of the early centuries AD, and it does not contain the famous line [(19)](#r19). These texts are not a pharaonic archive. I already knew them well enough that some of what I take from them is my own reflection coming back.

The formula bears on the relation between an internal model and the world. Hold a shape in mind long enough and you can direct effort in the environment toward it, and if you cannot make the thing you make the thing that makes it, whether that is the grain against a hard season or the ritual sequence already set out for a death.

## From many shared forms to one

Local gods are local shared forms. Each is tied to a valley, a spring, a kin line, a season, and each is adequate to the group that produced it. This is the configuration the comparative record describes in small-scale societies: supernatural agents with limited powers and narrow concern for human conduct [(20)](#r20). As the scale of cooperation rises, a shape tied to one place cannot be taught to strangers who do not have the place. What large societies exhibit are gods who are more powerful, more knowledgeable, and far more concerned with how people behave [(20)](#r20)[(21)](#r21). I read that change as a compression, many local forms giving way to fewer forms of greater reach.

The pressure shows up well before anything durable came of it. In Enūma Eliš, Marduk is granted kingship by the assembled gods and then given fifty names, each with a destiny attached, fifty being the number that had belonged to Enlil, so the list transfers the headship of the pantheon and, with it, sets Babylon at the centre [(22)](#r22). Akhenaten went further in the fourteenth century BC, elevating the Aten, closing traditional temples, suppressing traditionalists, and eventually declaring the Aten sole; within a short time of his death the traditional cults were restored, the city was abandoned and quarried, and his name was struck from the king lists [(23)](#r23).

The Israelite case took longer. Mark Smith's reconstruction has Yahweh beginning as one deity in a West Semitic pantheon, subordinate to El and later identified with him, with the worship of other deities continuing in Israel through the end of the Iron Age; prophets of the ninth and eighth centuries condemned Israel for worshipping others without denying that other nations had gods of their own [(24)](#r24)[(25)](#r25). The clearest monotheistic statements appear in the seventh and sixth centuries, and the fully universal formulation belongs to Isaiah 40–55, written in exile after the loss of land, king, and temple, with Assyria and Babylon as supranational powers, and with no further dependence on the place [(24)](#r24)[(25)](#r25).

What the compression did where it spread runs on three things.

The first is simplicity. The Christian creed and the Islamic testimony are each a sentence someone can carry after hearing it. Pantheons had been translatable into one another. Jan Assmann locates the decisive break in the distinction between true and false religion — true god against false gods, knowledge against ignorance, belief against unbelief — and argues that this distinction made it possible to exclude everything outside the truth [(26)](#r26).

The second is force. The Edict of Thessalonica of 27 February 380 required the peoples under Theodosius to hold the Nicene faith, named its adherents Catholic Christians, branded the rest heretics, denied their assemblies the name of churches, and promised imperial punishment; the laws of 391 and 392 extended the suppression to pagan worship as a crime [(27)](#r27). The Arab conquests of the seventh and eighth centuries carried the other branch across the same belt of the world in decades [(28)](#r28). Christianity had already spread for two centuries without an empire, on the simplicity of the confession.

The third is submission, in the ordinary sense that membership is constituted by it. *Islām* is the Arabic word for submission or surrender, and the tradition is named for the act [(29)](#r29); covenant obedience is the corresponding shape on the Judeo-Christian side. For those ruled without being inside the form, the arrangement that developed was a protected but legally subordinate position: non-Muslims retaining property, worship, and communal autonomy in exchange for acknowledging Muslim authority and paying the jizya, barred from bearing arms, with the standardized restrictions of the Pact of Umar consolidating later under the early Abbasids rather than during the conquests themselves [(30)](#r30)[(31)](#r31). The Christian version worked through law against the cults themselves.

It reached the parts of the world where the compression combined with a state that could enforce it.

On the direction of causation I differ from part of the literature I am citing. Ara Norenzayan's argument is that faith in watchful, punishing gods enabled cooperation among strangers and therefore made large groups possible [(20)](#r20), and the cross-cultural experiments do show that the more punitive and knowledgeable people rate their gods, the more they allocate to distant co-religionists [(21)](#r21). Peter Turchin's analysis of the historical sequence finds the strong causal arrow running the other way, from social scale to the appearance of these gods, with weaker feedback back from the gods to scale and only after societies are already large [(32)](#r32).

## Inference and prediction

This is inference across a record. I do not have access to the minds in question, only to what they left and to a reading instrument that the same history had a hand in shaping. I do not know that any of it is what happened, and I do not expect to, so the account has to be held to the predictions its confidence can support.
