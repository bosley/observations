# The Envoy

The Envoy is a projection of a self held ahead of the present one. It is drawn from a record, the journals and what can still be recalled, and from standards set for roles already occupied. It stays in front. In the ordinary sense an envoy is someone sent to represent a party they belong to, and judged by how fairly they carry the part. The roles are already being represented, chosen or not: family, employer, craft, the body that will have to be lived in later. Other people read that representative as the face of those things. The Envoy is the version that does it on standards set on purpose, under a name that can be asked a question when the present conduct is falling short. It is a working specification. It is not a fantasy and not a costume. If it never shows up in what gets done, it is a story about a person.

The practice that led to the name is older than the name. It began as a future-cast, a snapshot of who might be there in one to five years. The name came later. The tractate *Tractatus Principatuum* is the account this sits beside. An authentic self, in that text, is a mind holding the story it tells up to its hardened record, setting the order of its standards where an order would otherwise arrive by default, and acting on the difference (6.2). There is no record of a self that has finished that (6.21). The Envoy is one way of keeping a form in front of a self that cannot be completed.

## Early practice

I have been doing some version of this since I was about fifteen or sixteen. The snapshot was the part I could have picked up anywhere. Interviewers ask where you see yourself in five years, and the answer comes back as a litany of gross indicators someone wants to hit. That always felt disconnected, and disingenuous. I do not know where I got the future-cast. It was already being discussed in the culture around me, so I doubt it was mine.

What I was actually fighting was comparison. In high school I had problems with anxiety, and the anxiety ran on measuring myself against other people and seeing where I fell short. My family would console me by pointing the other direction: look at where you were a year ago. It did get through, and I still hold it. Who I can be, and who I should be, is read off what I value and what I think is important, and then off the work of becoming that. The growth indicators are positions relative to my own spot in time. Other physical entities nearby are the wrong measure.

## The journal

I kept journals because writing a thing down makes me confront how I feel about it. I picked the habit up and I still have it. It has been over fifteen years, close to twenty. There is a stack. The entries go from strife in a day, to getting through the day, to worries about the future.

Every few months, out of curiosity, I go back. I look at what I was feeling. I feel it again, and I feel it with the memory of how it actually played out. The old anxiety is there, and so is the later fact of it.

That second pass is what I mean by ironing the emotional landscape, in the sense the tractate gives those words. The landscape is the inner space in which a mind's notions exist and relate, its geography set by accumulated experience, and weight on a notion bends the notions nearest it (9.1, 9.12). I came to the rereading accidentally. The tractate's other method for that region is force, rituals meant to collapse the geometry and put new weight in its place (9.132). Mine was the slower one, going back over the page.

The continuous part was simple, and it was not a program. I recorded the worries. I wrote out who I wanted to be. I kept a loose guideline of things I should do. I did some active reflection, and that corrected my behavior and the record. It was beneficial. A lot of what used to give me anxiety does not. When something new does, I can lean on the earlier cases and expect that it will be fine.

I do not want this to look like a structured, intentional method. It is something I happened to do. I am curious whether a variant handed to someone else as a structure would even work. A structure implies a rigor the practice did not have. What I actually did was closer to four or five times a month. A few pages. The large actions that bubble up first: over the last three weeks, these things happened. If something does not make the record, it does not make the record. The things most salient at the time are the ones that last, and they are typically the important ones.

After a year or so I go through it. A different colored pen can mark the update, or not. I write what became of the worries, how they played out. I ask questions on the page and come back later. The useful return is after enough time that I have forgotten I asked. That is usually when the answer is there.

The point of keeping the Envoy is to have an image of the self I want, taken from the record I have internally and the record in the journals. Once I can see how things play out, and once some of that landscape has been ironed, I can see which aspects would change if I kept my mind on them.

## Ritual without a visible result

People have trouble acting through a ritual when there is no specific, measurable, intentional result at the end of it. Working out is the ordinary case. Real effects do come in. They come in so slowly that the mind has nothing it can visualize as a track. What it watches instead is a number, body weight, and weight fluctuates. At the beginning of a loss it is a poor measure.

The same trouble shows up in practices that verge on the religious, religious in the sense of being done frequently, for the sake of the rite, and not in the sense of being offered to an entity. What is being played out is a drama, an allegory the person is participating in. The tractate treats an allegory as a sequence of relations that has to be gone through, acts and distances and consequences, before a shape is outlined, and the outline is left in the emotional landscape by that repetition (15.2, 15.41). One emblem does not yield it.

The habit around me is utilitarian enough that an action which costs energy, and which does not feed something back that the observational mind can capture, contrast against a previous state, and see as growth, gets proclaimed non-factual and unimportant. I take that to be an error of multidimensional proportions. My first instinct is to snap that people are being lazy and stupid, and some of it is laziness and stupidity. The snap does not cover the picture. Sometimes it only looks lazy. Sometimes it only appears stupid. I know the feeling from the inside. When I am the one doing the slow thing, I feel stupid and I feel lazy, so that is the feeling I bring to it when I see it in someone else.

A person needs to see the progress. Attention, even in an ideal case where it has not been adulterated by chemicals or by the dopamine of little black squares and TikTok reels, may still not be enough. The brain may not be able to see the whole picture across the span of time it takes to witness it, or to fathom the results. And I flatten as I say that. "Fathom the results" treats the results as an instance in which everything is completed. That is not the case. The things go on until death. What sits outside the self goes on after that. On that picture, the notion of having a result is already wrong, and the process is one degree harder to justify, because if there is no such thing as a result then what is the point of the process.

That question, why I do anything at all, is the one the tractate takes up as finding meaning. When I am deciding where the energy goes, what I find myself asking is what I will get for doing it (8.01). The consequence this kind of work leaves is often not one I feel while I am in it (8.41). There is no record of a self that has finished the refining. Mitigation, keeping more of the shaping from going through unexamined, is itself the fruit. Nothing is obliged to show a second reward behind it. If I will not move until I am shown one, the nearest unexamined standard has already set the rate (8.6).

## Attributes and the mask

What I used to do, inside the snapshot, was list attributes I wanted society, and myself, to stick onto me. An academic achievement was on that list as a social attribute and an internal one together. The worth of the achievement, if there is no internal change that reflects it, is hard to find. The badge is an indicator of the thing. It is not the thing.

There is a counter-pull I also feel, and that a lot of people feel. What is the point of the achievement if it is not seen. Doing the work and having none of it show on the social mask is a real objection, and it is mine too. I can go on the internet and learn anything, to varying degrees, and still be unable to contribute to it in a way that counts, because contributing asks for social credentials. The credentials are how I would communicate to other people that I have experience-based authority. The tractate's description of the mask is the same bind from the other side: once another mind reads me as inside a role, I am, to that mind, the concept of that role, and fairness in how I carry it is the part still mine to choose (5.151, 16.01).

So the attributes I listed were social, and they required an air of internal authenticity. Without that air the endeavor was pointless to me.

## The projection

Over time the list stopped being the concept, and the name Envoy went to a self that exists ahead of me, one I want to become, held as a dynamic projection of self. The earlier shape cast that projection down onto the record as results, a state to arrive at. What I hold now is a snapshot that stays in front of me. It is like an event horizon I am falling forward toward, and I will not reach it. It is the edge of what is possible given the possibilities around me. It is realistic, and it is beyond what I would do by just floating.

I have considered modeling it further, as something I could put questions to and take answers from. I question the value of doing that. At best it would be my subconscious reflected back to me from that vantage, and I do not know that the outputs would be valid. They might be second-order valid, in the sense that what comes out of the contemplation can still yield something toward reaching the Envoy.

## Parts, standards, and the cone

Set down as a specification, the Envoy has four parts. A name, short enough to say, and usable as "what would the Envoy do here." Standards, for each role, stated so that someone else could tell whether they were met. An order of precedence for when the standards collide. A record that the thing is being lived.

A state, the five-year snapshot, is silent about the middle, where two obligations conflict or where there is no motivation. The form is what I can consult in the moment. A state says a target weight. The form says someone who eats like this, trains like this, and does not negotiate with the late craving. The order of precedence is decided once, while calm, so it does not have to be improvised tired. It need not be a strict rank. It can be conditional: health emergencies and the needs of dependents override deadlines, and deadlines override optional learning.

Asking the question under the Envoy's name puts a step between the question and my own name, which carries the history and the excuses. Work on third-person self-talk, including Ethan Kross's, and studies in which children persist longer at a boring task after pretending to be a persevering character, are the findings I lean on for that distance. Identity-based motivation, as in Daphna Oyserman's work, is the other finding I lean on: people persist more when the action feels like what someone like them does. "I am trying to lose weight" is a project. "The Envoy does not skip the session" is a trait being practiced. A five-year snapshot expires on arrival. The form is still available after that date has passed. The research is real and it is simplified here. Effect sizes vary. Some of the costume effects have replicated poorly. I treat the findings as reasons to try the practice, and I let the record say what actually works.

Making one runs in a certain order. I inventory the roles whose fruit I am: parent, partner, practitioner, teammate, and the body I am responsible for. Some were placed, born into or already on me, and some were chosen. Placed roles still get standards. The tractate makes the same split, and the two do not stay fixed: a placed membership can later be kept on purpose, and a chosen one can harden until setting it down is no longer something the record shows as available (8.22, 8.23). For each role I write one standard in observable terms. "Be a good parent" cannot be checked. Present at dinner three nights a week, phone away, and one unhurried conversation with each child each week, can. Then the order of precedence. Then a name, and a posture of three to five sentences: how it speaks under pressure, how it treats mistakes, how it treats other people. Temperament is in that. An Envoy that is only productive is brittle.

The check on whether the specification can be lived is borrowed from the light cone. Draw time upward and space sideways. From any event, the future light cone is the set of events that can still be influenced, limited by the fact that nothing travels faster than light. The past light cone is every event that could have influenced this one. Borrowed as a planning shape, my future cone is what I could realistically become from this body, this time, these obligations, these skills, and the rate at which I can actually change. The cone widens with time. What I can reach in thirty days is narrow. What I can reach in five years is wider. The slope is a personal speed limit, how fast effort turns into capability.

A viable Envoy sits inside that cone, near the boundary, a stretch a disciplined version of me could cross. One that requires eighteen hours of daily focus, or no sleep, or a personality replaced in ninety days, cannot be reached from where I stand, and I will feel the gap as failure. I also trace the target's past cone: which conditions, skills, and habits have to exist for the Envoy to be real at five years, at one year, at ninety days, and whether my present self sits inside that path. If it does, the path is real. If it does not, the target is outside the cone or a prerequisite is missing, and I add a nearer step. A large change in health or skill is often unreachable in a month and still inside five years, because each step moves me to a position from which the next is possible. A credential that looks impossible in one term can be a sequence. Each step widens the cone. What lies outside today's cone may lie inside next year's.

Two ways of drawing it go wrong. An oversized cone ignores constraints, family time, health, money. A sandbagged cone is drawn so close that it demands nothing, because it was drawn from fear. I draw the cone from the record, and I try not to draw it from how I feel on the day I draw it.

The record itself is a log: date, the standard tested, what I actually did. I review it weekly. The question at the review is what the record shows. Feeling like the Envoy has no part in it. Every quarter I adjust the standards and the cone from new evidence. An Envoy that never updates has become a story.

The whole Envoy is an asymptote, approached and not reached, which is the event horizon under this other likeness. Each time I close some of the distance, the picture gets finer and the guidepost moves, so there is no arrival at which I go slack. The next act still has to sit inside the cone. If the next act feels unreachable, the guidepost has become a reproach, and I add a nearer step.

The page I keep for a given Envoy holds the name, the posture, the roles and their observable standards, the order of precedence, the cone at ninety days and one year and five years together with what has to be true first, the mantras and their triggers, two or three questions I will actually ask and when I will ask them, where the record is logged and how often, and a quarterly review date.

## Etching and consultation

A mantra here is a short phrase repeated until it stops being something I say and becomes something that fires. Etching is the tractate's word for the same repetition: a relation passed across further spans of attention until it can be found later as imprint, without going back through the senses (12.121). Habit research is what I use for the ordinary mechanism. Behaviors become automatic through repetition in a stable context. Phillippa Lally's often-cited study found an average of about sixty-six days to automaticity, with a wide range, roughly eighteen to two hundred and fifty-four days, depending on the person and the behavior. The mantra is a compact cue that brings the whole Envoy up at the moment of choice. Retrieving it again also strengthens it, which is the testing effect.

Repetition by itself fails, and it can make things worse. In Wood, Perunovic, and Lee (2009), people with low self-esteem who repeated "I am a lovable person" felt worse, apparently because the sentence contradicted their evidence. A mantra the record does not support gives the mind something to argue with. The illusory truth effect cuts the other way: familiarity makes a phrase feel credible, which helps when the phrase is accurate and damages when it is flattery. So the phrases I keep are behavioral, present-tense, and anchored to something I have already done. "The Envoy shows up first" over "I am unstoppable." Process over an identity I cannot yet back.

I pair them with a trigger, the if-then planning Peter Gollwitzer calls implementation intentions, which outperform intentions left alone. When I open the fridge at night, I say the Envoy closes the door. When a task feels too deep to start, I say one more relation, and I begin. Three to six words, easy to say aloud, rhythmic if I can get that. Said aloud when I can, because speech engages more of me than thought alone. When a mantra goes dead, repeated on autopilot until it is noise, I retire it or sharpen it.

A few I actually use, or adapt:

- Fair to every role.
- Record over feeling.
- Start the fathom. Spend attention on purpose. A fathom, in the tractate, is the span attention takes (10.12, 10.2).
- Stretch, don't leap. Stay inside the cone.
- The Envoy already did the next thing.

In practice the Envoy works better as a person I can consult than as the specification alone: the me of the future, in enough detail that I can ask it, marked a little deeper each time I spend attention on it. On a choice in front of me, I take the present as if it were already the past.

I pause on the choice and name it: whether to skip the session, start the hard module, answer the message. I step to the Envoy's position, a year on, looking back at this moment. I ask what the record will show here, not what I feel like. I act, and I log it. The line counts because of what I did.

Prospective hindsight, imagining an outcome has already occurred, is cited as a way people identify what will matter and what can go wrong. Work on future-self continuity, Hal Hershfield and colleagues, finds that people who feel more connected to their future selves tend to choose in favor of them. I treat both as support for a practice, and I do not take them as proof of the mechanism. Effect sizes vary.

The talk can be in a journal, aloud, or silent for a few minutes. Either way the Envoy gets real questions. It is 9 p.m. and I am tired: what would you do with this hour. Which roles have I neglected this week, and what would the fair move be. What did the record actually show today. I am afraid of this problem: what is the smallest honest step. What am I telling myself that the record does not support. When an answer surprises me I write it down. Those are often the useful ones.

This is the part I remain unsure of. Playing questions and answers against the projection may only hand me my subconscious reflected back from that vantage, and I do not know that I should trust the outputs. The narrower use is the one I trust more easily: the contemplation can still show me something about how to move toward the Envoy, whether or not the voice I seem to hear is a valid report from a future self. I keep the tone a mentor's, someone who has walked the road and remembers that it was hard. If the voice is contemptuous, it has become a punishment persona and I rewrite it. I hold it as a likeness, a tool and a voice. Speaking with it is how I consult my own standards and my own imagination. I do not take it for a second being, and I do not let it replace the record. A vivid dialogue that leaves no trace in what I do is a story about a self.

## Where it fails

If the name mostly makes me feel small, the Envoy has become a punishment, and I redesign it toward a person I would respect and want to become. If I use the persona to avoid the record, it is an escape, and the gap between story and record is what it was supposed to close. Missing the mark is ordinary, and the record is allowed to show the miss. What matters is whether the miss gets folded back in. No Envoy is completed. Precedence rules are guides. When one of them produces an absurd result I revise the rule. If a month passes and nothing in the log has changed, the Envoy is decoration. I shrink the next step until doing it is undeniable, and then I do it.
