One reason I suspect that people sit uncomfortably with the contemplation of God/Not God is that the person sitting has a view of what God is. If you can fully articulate what God is, then you do not know God. It is easy to confuse the groups of "people who are not comfortable in the abstract" and "people who attribute to God some perceived wrongdoing." Someone who refuses to acknowledge an idea and someone incapable of understanding an idea are nearly indistinguishable in the downstream reasonings employed in areas such as justice and ethics, with exception of the flavor of contempt in which they deploy, with the former often able to leverage a larger lexicon.

The God/Not God stage of argumentation is tired, and I believe, ultimately is not even the core issue that the humans are grappling with. When all veils are dropped the core-most question to consider is "Why is there something rather than nothing," that is to say "Why does anything exist/Why do I exist." It's possible that the engine of "why" in our biology is applying the tool to an inappropriate context. The simple question of "Why" hides the notion that "there need be a reason." For many centuries we (humans) had a panoply of abstractions we could don to answer this on our behalf, and we could puppet our carcass around as an implementation of the transcendent ideals that we adopted.

Modern culture is filled with so many distractions and distorted senses of identity that people don't often have a chance to gather their attention as their own. From the moment we are born (American perspective) we are given an onslaught of data from a culture that has lost all sense of identity and has turned into a self-indulgent materialist cesspit of wasted potential.

The virus of victimhood infects even the most affluent of us. Look at modern celebrities. How many of them have everything in the world, yet they feel the need to leverage their family's internal state of affairs for the sake of pushing socio-political ideals? Perhaps there is nothing wrong with this in principle. Perhaps it's perfectly fine to allow the public eye in, and welcome commentary on the most intimate aspects of your child's development. Perhaps the feedback your child gets that drives them to attempt reforming their material condition to one of some abstract perfection under the edge of a knife and threat of harm is "progress."

I think that the reason for this state of absurdity and degeneration is not as nefarious as some would like to push and claim. I think it's more insidious than that of a shadow group attempting to cause our culture harm, though I can see how from some perspectives it would appear that way. I think that the core is simply that our culture is existentially exhausted. Every aspect of our being is pulled apart in a multitude of directions every minute of almost every hour of nearly every day. Humans are now "more connected" than ever, but the distance between them has never appeared greater. It's almost as if having the popular knowledge of news and events being thunked down into a digital consumable and fed directly to us has caused us to get pictures of reality that, while perhaps even true, by the nature of the process of thunking will misconstrue reality or regard it out of context to make some orthogonal point.

The average person in our culture gets swept when the Overton window moves because their core-most self-image has not yet been justified against their held beliefs about why they in particular exist. That unanswered why changes why they think others exist, and that is what supplies the oughts they treat as positions.

I think that the God/Not God argumentation is the same as the something/nothing argumentation, and it's one that most people never honestly contemplate. Most will take on a worldview before anything. In fact most people use a worldview to establish enough of a self to even then begin considering "what a worldview is." The picture of why anything exists is implied by worldview, and often it's the worldview that the individual was bootstrapped with. This causes a mess. People will argue justice and ethics from a view of existence that not only did they not choose, but they can't even see. It's no wonder then that the downstream reasoning comes out confused.

I also suspect radical extremism comes from this process under pressure. A person adopts some implied view. Then life corners them into looking at it. If their identity or their social world cannot survive the revision, they double down. They will do it past the moral hold-ups that still stop other people in the same culture. If morality is relative, those hold-ups are not a common brake. They only work when a person's life can afford them.

Ideally, the self should be one that does not have to be revised by the question of why there is anything. "God did it" is just as fine an answer as "it just happened" as long as the consideration of the ramifications of your unprovable claim is internally decided and able to supply the underpinnings of the self's worldview.
