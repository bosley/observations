In *The Archetypes and the Collective Unconscious* (Collected Works 9i), Jung sets a personal unconscious of forgotten or repressed complexes against a deeper layer that is inborn and the same in everyone. The contents of that collective unconscious "are known as archetypes": archaic, primordial types, "universal images that have existed since the remotest times." The archetype itself stays unconscious and irrepresentable, like a biological pattern of behavior. What appears is the image it takes once it becomes conscious, colored by the person in whom it appears.

Jung never fixed a set of twelve. The familiar list was built later, chiefly by Carol Pearson in *Awakening the Heroes Within* (1991) and then, with Margaret Mark, in *The Hero and the Outlaw* (2001).

1. Innocent
2. Explorer
3. Sage
4. Hero
5. Outlaw (also Rebel)
6. Magician
7. Everyman (also Regular Guy/Gal, Everyperson)
8. Lover
9. Jester
10. Caregiver
11. Creator
12. Ruler

Mark and Pearson group them by motive: independence (Innocent, Explorer, Sage), mastery (Hero, Outlaw, Magician), belonging (Everyman, Lover, Jester), and stability (Caregiver, Creator, Ruler).

He suspected the forms were hereditary, already present in the germ-plasm, and as old as the human species. If they ever began, he wrote, that beginning "must have coincided at least with the beginning of the species." A child is not a blank slate. It meets the world with inherited aptitudes, the way an animal meets it with instincts.

What is inherited is not a finished picture or idea, but the possibility of one: empty forms, "mentally expressed instincts." The content is filled in by a particular life. He compared the form to the lattice of a crystal, which fixes the shape the crystal can take without itself being a crystal. The image dreamed or mythologized is that form once experience has given it color. The origin he pointed to is biological, the psychic side of instinct.

He never closed a set of six either. Later readers pull six figures out of the ones he treated as structural, plus the wise old man, whom he listed among the chief images (with the shadow, the child, the mother, the maiden, the anima, and the animus). The ego is the center of consciousness only. These six are the figures around it.

1. Self. The whole psyche, conscious and unconscious together: the center of that whole and also its circumference. Often a circle, a square, a mandala. It shows up as a concern once the other figures have already taken shape, typically toward midlife.
2. Shadow. The hidden, repressed, mostly inferior and guilt-laden personality, the underside of whatever face is being worn. It also holds instincts, realistic reactions, and impulses the conscious attitude had no room for.
3. Persona. The social mask. A compromise with society, built to make an impression and to conceal. Collective, not the person. A life wears more than one, by age, status, and office.
4. Anima. The inner feminine in a man. His image of woman, and his own contrasexual possibilities. The inner attitude, a bridge toward the collective unconscious, where the persona is the bridge outward.
5. Animus. The inner masculine in a woman. The same office on the other side.
6. Wise old man. The archetype of spirit, or meaning. He appears when guidance is needed and conscious thought has run out. Jung met his own as Philemon. The corresponding feminine figure in his writing is closer to the mother than to a separate "wise old woman" slot.

## Through the tractate

Jung was meeting forms. A form is a shape outlined by relations, and none of the relations is the shape (12.0). He met those shapes as persons — a mask, a hidden twin, an inner woman or man, an old man who knows, a whole greater than the "I" — because that is how a form shows once attention is on it and the speech on hand is the speech of persons. The recurrence he could not get from one life is already described two ways: a shape the vessel can hold because of how the substrate is built, and a shared form put where a later mind can still find it.

## Empty form, filled image

He was careful that the archetype is empty. A formal possibility, "mentally expressed instinct," colored once it becomes conscious in a particular person. The likeness he used was the lattice of a crystal, which fixes the shape a crystal can take without itself being one.

That is the seed in the evolution-of-forms note. A repeated pattern in the substrate means certain outlines are available in any human mind. The vessel is that shape: biology and etch set what the quantity sits in (14.1). His archetypal image is the form once a reader has passed relations through it. More relations, finer resolution (12.01). The image is the etch (12.121). The archetype, as he defined it, is the outline before that passing.

## Two hosts

He housed the recurrence in the germ-plasm and called the housing the collective unconscious. The tractate splits the housing.

One host is the vessel. Separated groups arrive at similar shapes because the substrate makes those shapes findable. That is the heredity claim, kept as a conjecture.

The other host is the remnant. A form several minds carry is gone when they die unless it has been put into stone, into a story told the same way twice, or into a sequence of acts (18.0, 18.1). It sits in the social remnant and in the physical remnant. Ritual is that sequence installed ahead of a fracture (18.31). He read ritual as collective contents handed outward from the unconscious. Friction and scale produce the form, the form is put outside the single mind, and then it acts back (19.2). Etched past the point anyone is tracking it (4.32), it drives conduct without recall (3.4). From inside one mind that driving has no autobiography. It feels inherited. Much of what he called never individually acquired is imprint that landed before there was an "I" who could examine it, carried in by the tongue and the principality (2.2, 9.3, 21.5).

The crystal is the availability. The shared form is the particular gods, myths, and dream-figures that match across a region. He had one bin for both because he was standing at the scale of the single psyche. The same arrangement appears above the body and below it (4.34). Taking the scale you are standing on for the whole pattern is the scale error (7.4). The collective unconscious is that error given a place: the temperature of a principality (19), and weight that is a property of a region across many minds (9.2), encountered from inside as a layer under the personal memories.

Older names for this were a spirit over a people (19.3). The name points at what feeds the pattern and what it does to conduct. Taking the spirit for a second being set beside the membership is the move at 4.341. The figures are real as outlines.

## The six

Persona. Once another mind reads you as inside a principality, you are, to that mind, the concept of the principality as they hold it, like a mask (5.151). A role is that concept when membership is foregrounded (6.05). Wearing is the same act from the other end (16.01). He named the actor's mask and stopped at the compromise with society. The mask is whose concept it is: the principality as someone else holds it. A life has several because a mind sits in several principalities, and only some are in the foreground at once. Identification with the persona, the person who is nothing but the office, is the consistent self (6.3). The story matches the record, and the order of standards was supplied by whichever principality was nearest (6.17).

Shadow. Recall tells the story. Imprint drives, and the recalled story often has no account of it. Inscription error is taking the story for the whole of what is driving (7.3). Weight is the stored potential; the subject in front of you is the key, not the source (9.0, 9.01). Persona and shadow come out as twins because they are the two records of one principality: what other minds are reading you as, and what the soft system has been organizing along lines laid down before you were tracking them. The traits in the shadow that are ordinary instinct, a realistic reaction, a creative impulse, are relations etched and then left out of the role. Projection is wearing plus that key. A body is taken as wearing a notion, and a magnitude comes out that the body did not store.

Anima and animus. A shared form for the contrasexual relation, coarse or fine by how many relations are in what you take the other to be wearing. Love and lust, as stipulated, are the two ends of that run: the other held as a mind that could be fathomed, or held as an aspect (16.02). He met the same form from the inside, as an image of woman in a man or of man in a woman, and as the expectation brought before the person arrives. The image is placed before it is chosen (8.22, 2.2). It is also a shape the vessel can hold, which is why it shows up in dreams and myths the person was never taught as a theory. The persona is how you are legible to the group. The anima or animus is how a group-scale outline of the other sex is legible inside one landscape. Both are remnants.

Wise old man. A hard moment arrives, conscious speech is not adequate to the link, and what arrives is an authoritative voice in the person's own language. In the oldest strata of the Iliad that voice is a god, and to hear it is already to obey. The evolution-of-forms note takes that direction and declines the implication of malfunction: the gods there are what a process looked like from the inside before there was anything to call a self. He met his own as Philemon and called the class the wise old man, the archetype of spirit, there when guidance is needed and thinking has run out. That delivery is a principality, or the temperature of one, speaking at a scale the person does not read well from inside (19.5). A priest is the mind that can hold several accounts of one event and say what it requires (18.4). The old man is the figure put on that function so a single flame can relate to it. Taking Philemon for a second being beside the relations is the same miss as taking a spirit over a people for a second being beside the membership (4.341).

Self. His Self is the whole the ego is not: center and circumference, conscious and unconscious together. The self in the tractate is the "I" the mind understands itself to be, of both modalities (6.0). An authentic one is the mind currently holding the story to the hardened record, setting the order of standards the nearest principality would otherwise supply, and acting on the difference (6.2). That work is what he called individuation. An authentic self cannot be a completed self, because there is no record yet of a self that has finished (6.21).

The mandala is a likeness of emergence. One fathom is not a self. Self is what a run of fathoms does, the way temperature is what a lot of atoms do together (10.11–10.15). The mandala is a shared form for that whole, a portable outline none of the parts is. The sense that the "I" is orbiting something is the same reduction as the Primordiant: another event has not ended the record, and another member of the group has not held the instant (11.41). He stood inside one psyche, so the superset arrived as an inner figure. The tractate stands where the record runs out, points at a ground the fathom is of, and at 20.41 keeps the likeness to the Father in the collective emotional landscape as a likeness. Promoting the inner figure to the thing the likeness was of is contact error (7.1, 4.341).

## Compression

Local gods are local shared forms, each adequate to a valley or a kin line. As the principality gets larger, a form tied to one place cannot be taught to a stranger, and the forms that survive are fewer, of greater reach, and concerned with how a body behaves toward people it will not meet again (18.6). He collected the survivors — mother, child, shadow, wise old man, maiden, anima, animus — and because they recurred in patients who had not read each other's myths, he concluded the outline was in the seed. Some of that is the substrate conjecture. The rest is the compression. A figure already made portable shows up in dreams, in psychoses, and in religions with no memory of the borrowing, and it looks inherited.

The twelve attached to his name later are a further compression: twelve motives, each coarse enough to carry after one hearing. A shared form about shared forms. They are not the structural recurrence he was looking at.

## The filter

His sentence runs one way. The empty form picks up its color from the person it becomes conscious in. The next pass runs the other way. Once the flame has gone through that form, later observation is lit by it. Forms already known get a different shade line (12.22), and relations that had not been passed become passable.

A hide worn to come into tune with an animal is that filter on the body. It is a remnant of a form: a way of attending, a distance, what counts as food or threat or kin. Put it on and the reader who walked in is not the reader spending the fathom. Timber, herd, weather, a sick body: the objects need not change. The light does. Territory is not sitting there as empty space (9.11). It gets outlined because attention went through those relations.

The two passes are one etch. The person colors the animal-form, which is why a bear carried by one people is not the bear carried by another. Then that colored form colors whatever the flame is put on next. Repeated, it does not stay a costume. Familiarity is the relation meant again without being built from the start, and eventually without the skin (14.3). "In tune with the spirit" is that second state. The spirit-name is how the pattern gets pointed at (19.3). The wearing is how the pattern gets into the soft system (15.411). The rite works as a change in the reader.

This mask faces the other way from the persona. The social mask is worn so other minds read you as the principality. The animal is worn so you read an already-known ground as that form reads it. One changes the fruit they record. The other changes the light cast, including the light on the story told of the self. For the span of the rite you are the concept of that animal as the people hold it (5.151). Whether the new relations entered conduct, or only the picture did, is on the record after the hide comes off (6.12).
