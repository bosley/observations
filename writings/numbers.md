I grab a handful of dolomite off the ground in front of my porch and toss the rocks onto the decking. I ask my 2 year old to cound them. With the most adorable drawn-out vowels he counts "Ooooooooone, Twooooooo, Threeeee!" There were 5, but by the time he had collected the three rocks for himself his mind was locked onto something else entirely and he begins to babble about trucks. 

Before I had grabbed the rocks I knew not which of the rocks were selected, what their measurements would be, or the count that would be present when laid out on a surface. My only motivation was to gather a small enumerable collection to exercise my son's counting and to start him off on a thought-train. 

He only contains a degree of understanding of numbers. He loosly maps the symbols of "1 2 3 4 5 6 7 8 9 0" to different concepts, to which i suspect time and sequence is the primary factor. I don't believe he has an understanding of "3" the same way that most adults do, as he is only 2 years of age.

Most people struggle with numbers, i suspect, because they are locked into a modality of thought that lack either the ability or happeneing "to see" the forms required to illuminate the higher-order state of affairs.

The prompt for this one asks whether "three" needed someone who can mean three. That is the same question I am asking from the porch, just aimed at him instead of the rocks. As a parent I am in deep consideration of what concepts to introduce to my son, and when. Instead of thinking "he should know X by Y time" I think "what is the extend of his abilities at the moment, and how can I introduce new concepts through play and example. 

As a parent I am aware of many times I have missed the mark and shown, through example, behaviors that if I saw repeated from my child, I would find utterly unacceptable if immitated outside of very specific contexts.

For instance, consider raising a dog along with a toddler. If the dog misbehaves and you have to raise your voice to gather it's attention, the toddler may see raising ones voice as a way to express any feelings of unrest or discontent (I suspect a mix of what they were feeling in the moment and how they understood me to be feeling given their limited `emotional breadth and depth with regards to their capacity for understanding`.) And I think that is also how "three" gets into a person, out of someone meaning it at him over and over until he can mean it back. The rocks only had to stand there and be countable.

Similar to the distance between my 2 year old and I there exists a nausiating distance between myself and others. The usage `above/below` as verbiage here tends to introduce concepts of ego in the dimensionality that I want to avoid. Its of no social or other hierarchical import. It is simply a distance. Sometimes its in `capacity`, but in my life it seems to be more in `familiarity`. 
There simply exist too many fields in which one may totally indulge themselves intellectually, and sometimes the height of one's biological capabilities simply aren't expressed as they lack the motiviation for the pursuit of either breadth or depth within their fields of interest.

For instance, consider a college professor. Someone with a PhD in Geology may indeed be the world's most incompetent computer end-user. They may have reached the edge of human understanding in a field, helped defined the forefront, but still express feelings of utter dismay when they plug in a digital device into one computer and for some reason unexpected to them, it doesn't appear on a totally different computer (a real story from my time in I.T.) 
Their `capacity` was large, and they used it primarily on depth of a specific subset of fields that we loosly categorize as "geology." It didn't suprise me then, when I explained what happend, he understood. The type of person he was didn't feel upset about the information, he simply adapted to the new information. He just wasn't `familiar` with things that we would assume "everyone knows" in this era.

He means three as well as I do, though. The gap between him and the machine is the same shape as the gap between my son and "three": `capacity` present, practice absent. The only difference is which fields the practice showed up in. 

Similarly, someone who lives and works in a rural area may have the same `capacity` but through some degree of choice have opted to live a lifestyle where their capacity is spread amongst varying breadths and depths of various fields required of their environment. The top performers of useful output with repspect to resource consumption within their community expressed over time, in my experience, are of the same hollistic intellectual measure as the top performers of the same measure in academia. Of course this is suspicion, and we would need to look at it on equal terms to validate it, but I don't think its a bold claim to say that average humans in this western culture will have similar intellectial capabilities and that the degree to which they are accessed/developed is dependent on the "need" to access/develop them.

So, back to the decking. What is on the deck is five rocks. What is not on the deck is three. Counting is one of the few fields where everyone in this culture gets the practice early and keeps it, which is probably why three looks like it was with rocks the whole time, though I saw no "3" present. I saw the three as a member of five, and he discovered each of the "three" as he counted them out. 

In a way, we saw entirely different numbers, in another way they were the same.
