
Everything in the world is too much to discern truth from. Everything is a series of rabbit holes that lead down studies of histories or the performance of mental exercises to engrain rules of shared systems to express abstract relations rooted in the realm of logic, inferred from geometry (history and maths).

Mathematics is the form of communication you can check by working the relations again, so you do not have to trust the mind that sent them. The systems of maths stand in logics you can return to, with symbolisms invented to encompass those logics, and the receiving mind can step through a proof and see whether the relations stand correct. The symbolisms are still learned in language, from other people, and from conventions of notation, so mathematics has the same kinds of distortion as other communication. An expression can be written so the order of operations is not fixed by the notation, and two people who know the notation can each work it differently and each believe the rules were followed. sin²x is read as the square of the sine, or as the sine applied twice, depending on which convention was taught. Published proofs have later been found wrong. Whether the logics are discovered or invented is disputed, and that dispute does not have to be settled in order to check a proof. A model can also be applied to a system it does not fit. That misapplication is a real error, and working the relations again is how you look for it. History, testimony, and the transmission of emotion cannot be checked by working them again. Where other records exist, those can be compared with the statement.

A mastery of arithmetic will not fill most of us with the loving contentment of holding a newborn. The ability to describe the patterns emerging in the universe has none of the felt relatability that social cohesion and the transmission of emotion demand. What crosses in a proof, and what crosses in a sentence about holding a newborn, is the structure of relations. The experience that filled those relations stays with the person who had them, and the receiving mind supplies its own. Mathematics does not require the receiver to have lived the phenomena, because the relations are what is sent, and they can be worked. A sentence about holding a newborn relates in the sender’s sense only if the receiving mind already has experience of that kind to supply. If it does not, the mind supplies some other experience it does have, which means the structure has been transmitted and the contentment the sender was referring to has not. Some feelings seem impossible to communicate to a person who has not had them because of this. The relations can be stated in a clear sentence, and the experience those relations came from is still not in the transmission, so the receiving mind supplies experience it already has.

History, that is the written record produced by man in a multitude of languages over time, is riddled with distortion of multiple orders. A lie, as I mean it here, is the intentional recording of a mistruth. That is one order. The other orders are second-hand mentioning being recorded, transcription error, translation misunderstandings, vernacular that has shifted, and so on. Those are distortion, or noise. They do not require that anyone decided to deceive. Setting that taxonomy of error categories and degrees aside, it is hard to tell what is actually factual in the written record. Distortion does not remove every relation from a record. When the same relation appears in records that were not copied from each other, the agreement is evidence for that relation. When the records conflict, the conflict is evidence that distortion occurred in at least one of them. This is a weaker check than working a proof, because the events cannot be run again. A distorted record can still contain fact. Distance in time exacerbates the problem, but it is not only a problem of the distant past: even a document a week old can use vernacular whose everyday meaning has suddenly shifted because of events on social media, and then that document can be read as misinformation or propaganda when it was never trying to be either.

When I say “history,” I mean any set of symbols meant to encode information for a mind to read later. Even this document, as I write it, is becoming history. That is broader than the usual sense of the word, though it still includes that usual sense. Any written or recorded information meant to be read, understood, or interacted with by a mind is committed to history the moment it is created.

Those symbols are language, and language does not begin at the record. It forms the mind that will later try to read one.

A mind is constructed in language before it can examine what it has been given. Communication is learned prior to critical thought. Speech, gesture, and repeated conduct carry assumptions about the world, about selves, and about what counts as real, and those assumptions are impressed into the developing person through engagement with other beings. A new mind arrives without a personality. Family, vernacular, ritual, and routine bootstrap it, and the child takes up positions from the nearest people without the reasons that produced them. What crosses from one mind to another is the structure of relations. The experience that filled those relations stays with the person who had them, and the receiving mind supplies its own. In this way a mind comes to be made of forms, sets of relations it can feel and use, and the identity it recognizes as its own is carved out of forms it did not choose. When I say “forms,” I mean those sets as one mind holds them and can use them. They come from the speech, gesture, and conduct that mind was given before it could examine them.

Those forms arrive already bent and warped. Words used even slightly incorrectly hand different objects to the minds taking them in, or indeed the same if the recipient doesn’t have the correct understanding of the word used. This means that errors arrive in transmission and reception. When a mind is still learning language and executing it through mimicry, these errors in communication can build complex misunderstandings that are “near truths.” A near truth can be used in ordinary communication, so that communication does not force a correction, and the mind then applies the near truth to what it hears next.

Let’s take a look at the phrase “the door is unlockable.” Are we stating that the door cannot be locked, or that it is locked and we are unable to unlock it? Without the proper context around the phrase’s usage we can’t know. Most of the time the context does determine which was meant. Depending on the context, delivery, and emotional state of the mind when one of such a nebulous phrase is first utilized, the mind may make a multitude of misunderstandings. If this happens while the mind is still learning by mimicry, and there is no second account to check it against, the misunderstanding can stay as a near truth. “We need to have a talk” is a case where the surrounding words often do not correct the error. One mind can learn the phrase from people who use it to mean a request for information or an invitation to repair. Another mind can learn it from people who use it to mean a warning or a demand for an explanation. The sentences the phrase appears in can be clear for both. What is transmitted is the structure. The experience that fixed the use for each mind stays with that mind, and the other mind supplies its own. Later sentences are then read through the form already acquired, including sentences that are themselves clear. The two minds can talk coherently and be referring to different things. That near truth stays because it was acquired before the mind could think critically about it, and further use of the phrase is interpreted by the form, so the use does not provide a correction.

Those forms are not all of the same kind, and they are not all equally open to later correction. Some of them arrive so early, and shape so much of what the mind can notice, that ordinary communication never reaches them. The near truths that stay are not only local misreadings of a word or a phrase. Some of them settle the way the mind groups and organizes whatever it meets, so that later corrections can only operate inside the groupings already in place. I have been modeling the places these forms sit as simple layers. It seems that a person’s inmost layer is the ontological: the forms by which a mind organizes and groups what it meets so that each moment of reality can be made sense of at all. Those forms decide what counts as a thing, what counts as the same thing continuing, what counts as a change, and what categories of relations are even available to be noticed. A person’s answer to “why is there something rather than nothing,” to “when does human life begin,” or to “what constitutes a good act” sits inside this layer.

The answer can be one that they have really thought about and intentionally crafted, or it can be one nobody explicitly articulated, but through implication of other articulations, it was installed through inference. Either way it sets the character of the attention that goes out from there. Once those organizing forms are in place, there is a further layer for what counts as real, or good, or worth bothering about. Contingency against necessity sits there, and so does whether a thing has a worth of its own or only the worth someone arbitrarily assigns it. Those are forms the way the reading of “we need to have a talk” was a form. They are patterns the mind already has when it looks at the next thing.

Out from that are the moral forms: harm and fairness and authority and the rest of that family. Whether or not the mind treats those as applicable to the people immediately around them or regardless of the situation depends on things established by the earlier layers. What the form actually says still decides which way the mind goes. A mind with the same inner forms can still treat harm as the thing to avoid and loyalty as the thing to keep, and those will not point the same direction when the two come apart.

The layer after that is other people, the social layer. Attachment, standing, what is owed back, fighting, care. The “we need to have a talk” case is one of these forms. The sentences were clear for both minds. What each mind had already put under the phrase was not the same, and each of those forms was already being shaped by whatever sat further in—the experiences that the mind has gone through—so the two minds could keep talking and keep meaning different things without the talk forcing a correction.

Outermost is the everyday forms the mind actually uses when it attends to something. This is where the rabbit holes are, the studies of histories, the mental exercises, the rules of the shared systems. Most of what gets called learning happens here. A form further in is already deciding which of those rabbit holes look like they are worth going down.

If that inmost layer is never addressed, the mind fills in a default that makes sense from the people around it. The default is not an answer to any of the questions that sit inside the ontological layer. It is what those people were already treating as “the case.” Later, a dilemma shows up among the moral forms, and the strife is often the first time that the mind becomes aware of an etched-in presupposition. Sometimes the attention is on the ethical situation, the conclusion comes from what the group identity demands, and that demand carries an implication about one of the core questions inside the ontological layer, regardless if it is doing so explicitly.

Which leaves the question of how anyone discerns what is true, when the tools they have for telling what truth is may already have the error in them. Working the relations again will catch a miss in that outer layer, the way a second account catches the wrong reading of “the door is unlockable.” It will not catch a form that got put in by implication, because the tool being used to check is shaped by the layer the error went into. What I can grade is how far an account has come apart from the remnant. I have not observed a truth I could point at and call the thing itself. What gets called truth I treat as the far end of that grading, approached and not reached, and what I actually have are degrees of falsehood which can get smaller without my arriving there. The layers are part of why. A correction at the outer layer leaves the inner form where it was, and the inner form is what decides which relations get worked next.
