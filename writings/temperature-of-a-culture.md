We’re all familiar with the ancient stories of angels, demons, and other spiritual forces exercising “dominion” over nations and their peoples. I’m starting to think these aren’t just leftover superstition.

Instead, they look like an early, intuitive form of systems thinking that showed up long before we had the language of empiricism or science. I suspect they come from an evolved capacity to notice and name emergent patterns in collective human behavior. Most of us can catch a ball without understanding gravity and drag, or pick up on when someone is being deceptive without knowing anything about microexpressions. These stories seem to work the same way. They gave people a way to make sense of bigger group dynamics and actually talk about them.

When they named a particular demon or old deity — Baal, Moloch, the prince over Persia, whatever it was — it let them point at a pattern that had gotten self-reinforcing and dominant. Giving it a face and some lore meant they could discuss what fed it, what made it stronger, and what might push back against it. They didn’t have access to the modern concepts of group psychology or memetics or complex systems. They just had a working language for it.

Then the Enlightenment came along and put a kind of veil over this. Instead of wondering whether there might be something useful in the old ways of seeing, a lot of people moved straight to extreme empiricism. If it couldn’t be measured and written up and published somewhere official, it got treated like primitive nonsense. A fair amount of actual pattern recognition probably got discarded in the process.

When you look at it this way, a lot of the biblical and religious language about demons being given authority over nations starts to read like pattern recognition at scale. The same goes for the way some people today talk about a culture having “succumbed to Baal and Moloch.” It’s not usually a literal claim that some ancient spirit moved into the country. It’s more a recognition of a higher-order pattern that has emerged from a huge number of individual choices and behaviors — the same way temperature emerges once enough molecules are moving in a certain way.

Once that larger pattern exists, it starts acting back on the people inside it. It shapes what feels normal, what gets rewarded, and what becomes hard to resist. Naming it gives you a way to actually talk about something that would otherwise just feel like “that’s just how things are now.” But a lot of modern pseudo-intellectuals, especially the ones still nursing generational church hurt, seem determined to stay blind to it.
